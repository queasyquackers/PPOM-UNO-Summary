# Lecture #51: Differential Diagnosis of Memory Disorders



## 1. Evaluation of Memory Loss
**Story Mode:**
Not all memory loss is dementia. The first step is to distinguish between **Delirium** (acute, fluctuating, inattention) and **Dementia** (chronic, progressive). We must also rule out **MCI** (Mild Cognitive Impairment), where function is preserved despite memory complaints.

### 1.1 Delirium vs. Dementia
*   **Delirium:**
    *   **Time:** Acute onset (hours to days).
    *   **Course:** Fluctuating.
    *   **Key Feature:** **Inattention** and altered level of consciousness.
    *   **Cause:** Often underlying medical illness (infection, metabolic) on top of a vulnerable brain (dementia).
*   **Dementia:**
    *   **Time:** Chronic (months to years).
    *   **Course:** Progressive decline.
    *   **Key Feature:** Memory/Cognitive loss *without* altered consciousness (until late stages).

### 1.2 Reversible Causes
Before condemning a patient to a neurodegenerative diagnosis, rule out the fixable stuff:
*   **Metabolic:** Hypothyroidism, B12 deficiency.
*   **Structural:** Subdural hematoma, Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH).
*   **Infectious:** Syphilis, HIV.
*   **Toxic:** Alcohol (Wernicke's), Medications (Benzos, Anticholinergics).
*   **Psychiatric:** Pseudodementia (Depression).

## 2. Major Neurodegenerative Dementias
**Story Mode:**
Different dementias attack different parts of the brain, leading to distinct "signatures." Alzheimer’s hits memory (Hippocampus). FTD hits personality (Frontal lobes). LBD hits the visual cortex and motor system.

### 2.1 Alzheimer's Disease (AD)
*   **Epidemiology:** Most common cause. Age is the #1 risk factor.
*   **Genetics:**
    *   **Late Onset:** **APOE e4** allele (Risk factor, not causative).
    *   **Early Onset:** **APP**, **Presenilin-1**, **Presenilin-2** (Autosomal Dominant).
*   **Presentation:**
    *   Short-term memory loss (Hippocampus).
    *   Language deficits (Word finding).
    *   Visuospatial issues (Getting lost).
*   **Pathology/Imaging:**
    *   **MRI:** **Hippocampal Atrophy** (Mesial Temporal Lobes).
    *   **PET:** **Temporoparietal Hypometabolism**.
*   **Treatment:**
    *   **AChE Inhibitors:** Donepezil, Rivastigmine (GI side effects).
    *   **NMDA Antagonist:** Memantine (Moderate-Severe).
    *   **Anti-Amyloid mAbs:** Lecanemab/Donanemab (Risk of **ARIA**: Amyloid Related Imaging Abnormalities - bleed/edema).

### 2.2 Vascular Dementia
*   **Mechanism:** "Strokes" accumulating over time.
*   **Subcortical:** Slow processing speed, lethargy (small vessel disease).
*   **Multi-Infarct:** Step-wise decline.
*   **Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy (CAA):**
    *   Buildup of amyloid in vessel walls.
    *   **Presentation:** **"Amyloid Spells"** (Transient focal deficits mimicking TIA) and **lobar hemorrhages**.

### 2.3 Lewy Body Dementia (LBD)
*   **Core Features:**
    *   **Parkinsonism:** Rigidity, bradykinesia.
    *   **Visual Hallucinations:** detailed, often people/animals (e.g., Robin Williams case).
    *   **Fluctuations:** Good days/bad days.
    *   **REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (RBD):** Acting out dreams (loss of atonia).
*   **Treatment Caution:** **EXTREME sensitivity to Antipsychotics** (can cause severe rigidity/death). Avoid Benzos.

### 2.4 Frontotemporal Dementia (FTD)
*   **Presentation:**
    *   **Behavioral Variant:** Onset in 50s-60s. Disinhibition, apathy, loss of empathy, hyperorality (craving sweets), compulsive behaviors (Bruce Willis case). Memory is *spared* early on.
    *   **Primary Progressive Aphasia:** Language loss first.
*   **Genetics:** MAPT, GRN, C9ORF72.
*   **Management:** SSRIs for behavior. AChE inhibitors do NOT help.

## 3. Other Specific Etiologies

### 3.1 Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH)
*   **Triad:** **"Wet, Wobbly, Wacky"**
    *   Urinary Incontinence (Wet).
    *   Gait Apraxia / Magnetic Gait (Wobbly).
    *   Dementia (Wacky).
*   **Pathophysiology:** Enlarged ventricles comprising white matter tracts (legs/bladder fibers run medially).
*   **Diagnosis/Rx:** High Volume Lumbar Puncture (drain 30cc). If gait improves $\rightarrow$ **VP Shunt**.

### 3.2 Prion Diseases
*   **Mechanism:** Conversion of normal PrPc (helical) to abnormal **PrPsc** (Beta-pleated sheet) $\rightarrow$ rapid spongiform degeneration.
*   **Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease (CJD):**
    *   **Presentation:** Rapidly progressive dementia + **Startle Myoclonus** + Ataxia. Death < 1 year.
    *   **Diagnosis:** MRI (Cortical ribboning), CSF (**RT-QuIC** is gold standard; 14-3-3 is old/nonspecific).
*   **Fatal Familial Insomnia (FFI) - *The Mystery Case*:**
    *   **Alfredo's Case:** 53yo male with profound **insomnia**, dysautonomia (sweating, pinpoint pupils, tachycardia), and dream-enactment.
    *   **Course:** Thalamic degeneration prevents sleep $\rightarrow$ Death.

### 3.3 Chronic Traumatic Encephalopathy (CTE)
*   **Cause:** Repetitive mild TBI (Football, Boxing).
*   **Timeline:** Symptoms start years/decades after sports career.
*   **Presentation:**
    *   **Mood/Behavior:** Explosivity, aggression, suicide (Aaron Hernandez).
    *   **Cognitive:** Dementia later.
    *   **Motor:** Parkinsonism/ALS-like features.
*   **Pathology:** Tauopathy.

### 3.4 Wernicke's Encephalopathy
*   **Cause:** Thiamine (B1) deficiency (Alcoholics).
*   **Triad:** **Confusion, Ataxia, Ophthalmoplegia** (Nystagmus/Lateral Rectus palsy).
*   **Rx:** **IV Thiamine BEFORE Glucose** (to prevent precipitating Korsakoff's).
*   **Korsakoff's Psychosis:** Chronic irreversible amnesia + Confabulation (damage to mammillary bodies/thalamus).

:::highyield
**Dementia Buzzwords**
| Diagnosis | Buzzword / Key Feature |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Alzheimer's** | Hippocampal Atrophy, Amyloid Plaques/Tau Tangles |
| **LBD** | Visual Hallucinations, Parkingonism, Neuroleptic Sensitivity |
| **Frontotemporal** | Disinhibition, Hyperorality, Young onset (50s) |
| **NPH** | Wet, Wobbly, Wacky (Magnetic Gait) |
| **CJD** | Startle Myoclonus, Rapid progression |
| **Wernicke's** | Ataxia, Ophthalmoplegia, Thiamine |
:::

## 6. Review Questions
**1. A 72-year-old male is brought in by his family for evaluated of "acting strange." They report he has been talking to people who aren't there and sometimes thinks his wife is an imposter. On exam, he has mild rigidity in the upper extremities. Which medication should be AVOIDED in this patient?**

A. Donepezil
B. Carbidopa-Levodopa
C. Haloperidol
D. Memantine
E. Melatonin

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: C (Haloperidol)**

**Rationale**:
The patient has features of **Lewy Body Dementia** (Visual Hallucinations, Capgras delusion/imposter, Parkinsonism). LBD patients have **severe neuroleptic sensitivity**. Typical antipsychotics like Haloperidol can precipitate severe rigidity, catatonia, or even death.

</details>

**2. A 55-year-old woman is brought by her husband for personality changes. She has been making inappropriate sexual comments to strangers, eating only candy bars, and spending thousands of dollars on ceramic figurines. Her memory for recent events is relatively preserved. What is the most likely diagnosis?**

A. Alzheimer's Disease
B. Frontotemporal Dementia (Behavioral Variant)
C. Bipolar Disorder
D. Vascular Dementia
E. Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: B (Frontotemporal Dementia)**

**Rationale**:
This is the classic presentation of **Behavioral Variant FTD**: Early onset (<60), **Disinhibition**, **Hyperorality** (dietary changes), compulsive behaviors, and spared memory early in the course.

</details>

**3. A 68-year-old male presents with a "magnetic gait," where his feet appear stuck to the floor. He has also had several episodes of urinary incontinence and mild memory decline. A high-volume lumbar puncture is performed, and his walking improves significantly. What is the next best step in management?**

A. Start Carbidopa-Levodopa
B. Start Donepezil
C. Refer for Ventriculoperitoneal Shunt
D. Repeat Lumbar Puncture in 1 month
E. Start Finasteride

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: C (Refer for Ventriculoperitoneal Shunt)**

**Rationale**:
This patient has **Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus** (Wet, Wobbly, Wacky). The positive response to the large volume LP (diagnostic tap) predicts a good response to permanent CSF diversion via a **VP Shunt**.

</details>

**4. A 45-year-old female presents with rapid cognitive decline over the last 3 months. She has developed severe ataxia and startle myoclonus. An EEG shows generalized periodic sharp wave complexes. What is the gold standard diagnostic test for this condition?**

A. CSF 14-3-3 protein
B. CSF RT-QuIC assay
C. Brain Biopsy
D. Serum Ceruloplasmin
E. MRI with contrast

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: B (CSF RT-QuIC assay)**

**Rationale**:
This is **Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease (CJD)** (Rapid dementia, myoclonus, ataxia). While 14-3-3 was used historically, **RT-QuIC** (Real-Time Quaking-Induced Conversion) is now the specific gold standard test on CSF to detect the abnormal prion protein.

</details>

**5. Which of the following findings is most strongly associated with Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy?**

A. Subcortical lacunar infarcts
B. Lobar intracerebral hemorrhages
C. Tremor at rest
D. Early gait instability
E. Choroid Plexus Cyst

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: B (Lobar intracerebral hemorrhages)**

**Rationale**:
**Cerebral Amyloid Angiopathy (CAA)** involves amyloid deposition in the vessel walls of the cortex, making them friable. This leads to recurrent **Lobar Hemorrhages** (in the hemispheres, sparing deep structures) and "Amyloid Spells."

</details>
