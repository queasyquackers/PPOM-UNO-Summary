### Slide 10 (Score: 132.00)
```text
• All preganglionic efferent autonomic nerve fibers and somatic motor fibers to skeletal 
muscle, all parasympathetic postganglionic fibers and sympathetic fibers innervating 
sweat gland release the neurotransmitter acetylcholine – these are cholinergic 
neurons.
• Acetylcholine is synthesized in the cytosol from acetyl-CoA and choline and stored in 
vesicles. It is released into the synapse when an action potential reaches the nerve 
terminal and triggers sufficient influx of calcium ions via N-type calcium channels.
• Acetylcholine is degraded in the synaptic cleft by acetylcholinesterase. 
• Acetylcholine activates nicotinic and muscarinic receptors in the CNS and periphery: 
− preganglionic nicotinic receptors (ionotropic receptors)
− neuromuscular nicotinic receptors (ionotropic receptors)
− muscarinic G protein-coupled receptors (metabotropic receptors) on effector organs
Key points: Acetylcholine
```

```text
• Nicotinic receptors (nAChRs) are ligand-gated nonselective cation channels permeable 
to Na+ and K+; some are permeable to Ca2+. Two molecules of ACh binding at the 
interface between the  subunits and the adjacent subunits open the nicotinic receptor 
ion channel, permitting one or more specific ions to diffuse through. 
• Five subgroups of muscarinic receptors, mChRs, (M1-5) have been described. They 
are:
− M1, M3, M5 couple to the Gq/11PCR family of G proteins.
− M2, M4 preferentially couple to the Gi/oPCR family of G proteins.
• Cholinomimetic drugs – acetylcholine-like drugs – have numerous and complex 
actions. 
• A drug’s structure determines its relative selectivity for receptor type – muscarinic or 
nicotinic receptors – and resistance to hydrolysis – the structure-activity 
relationship.
```

### Slide 12 (Score: 112.00)
```text
• The direct-acting cholinomimetics are choline esters and naturally occurring alkaloids that 
bind specific receptor types and trigger an action potential or activate a second 
messenger signaling system of that receptor subtype. That is, they are nicotinic or 
muscarinic receptor agonists.
• The choline esters, which are quaternary ammonium compounds, are poorly absorbed 
from the GI tract and have limited ability to cross the blood-brain barrier. They are short-
acting and rapidly eliminated by the kidneys. 
• The alkaloids, such as nicotine and pilocarpine, are weak bases and tertiary amines. They 
are readily absorbed and cross the blood-brain barrier. They are metabolized by the liver. 
The metabolites are primarily eliminated by the kidney.
• The various agents are relatively nonselective and differ in their spectrums of action – that 
is, the degree of muscarinic versus nicotinic stimulation and in their relative affinities for 
the muscarinic receptor subtypes.
Key points: Properties of direct-acting cholinomimetics
```

### Slide 13 (Score: 225.00)
```text
• The various direct-acting cholinomimetics are used in the treatment of urinary bladder 
disorders, xerostomia, glaucoma, miotic agents, and in the diagnosis of bronchial 
hyperreactivity.
• Muscarinic toxicity is characterized by CNS stimulation (uncommon with choline esters, 
which do not cross BBB), miosis, ciliary muscle spasm (spasm of accommodation), 
bronchoconstriction, excessive gastrointestinal and genitourinary smooth muscle 
activity, increased secretory activity (sweat glands, airway, gastrointestinal tract, lacrimal 
glands), and vasodilation.
• Nicotinic receptor agonists are smoking cessation aids.
• Nicotine’s toxic effects include ganglionic stimulation and depolarizing blockade and 
neuromuscular end plate depolarization leading to fasciculations and then flacid 
paralysis. CNS toxicity includes stimulation (including convulsions) followed by 
depression and paralysis of respiratory muscles.
• Varencline is a synthetic neuronal α4β2 nAChR partial agonist that decreases nicotine 
cravings and withdrawal symptoms. It is well absorbed and minimally metabolized with 
excellent bioavailability.
Key points: Direct-acting cholinomimetics’ clinical uses and adverse effects
```
