```text
Basics of Demyelination
1. Loss of myelin (demyelination)
2. Relative preservation of axons
• Preservation of axons key to help distinguish 
demyelinating process from more nonspecific 
destructive process
• Both an infarct and a demyelinating lesion have loss 
of myelin and show foamy macrophages 
phagocytizing the debris.
• In an infarct, however, the axons will also be 
destroyed.
```

### Slide 10 (Score: 157.00)
```text
Robbins and Cotran, Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed., 2020, Ch. 28
• Available evidence: MS initiated by Th1 and Th17 T cells that react against 
myelin antigens and secrete cytokines 
• Th1 cells secrete IFN-γ, which activates macrophages
• Th17 cells promote recruitment of leukocytes
• Demyelination caused by these activated leukocytes and their injurious 
products 
• Infiltrate in plaques and surrounding regions of the brain consists of T cells 
(mainly CD4+, some CD8+) and macrophages
• Therapies being developed that modulate or inhibit T-cell responses and 
block the recruitment of T cells to the brain
• Contribution of humoral immunity suspected, based on oligoclonal bands of 
immunoglobulin in CSF
• MS appears to be an autoimmune disease triggered by an environmental 
factor in a genetically susceptible individual.
```

```text
Metachromatic Leukodystrophy 
• Autosomal recessive disease  - deficiency of lysosomal enzyme, 
arylsulfatase A, which cleaves sulfate from sulfate-containing lipids 
(sulfatides) as first step in their degradation

sulfatides; when they bind to certain dyes (e.g.,toluidine blue) sulfatides shift  
absorbance spectrum of dye, known as metachromasia 
• Similar metachromatic material detected in peripheral nerves and in urine – 
urine test sensitive method for diagnosis.
• Multiple clinical subtypes
```
