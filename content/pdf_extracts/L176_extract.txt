Normal Flora of the Skin
• Gram positives:
• Staphylococcus aureus (30-60%), S. epidermidis (~100%)
• Streptococcus pyogenes (Group A Streptococcus [GAS])
• Diptheroids: Corynebacterium and Propionibacterium spp.
• Gram negatives: Proteus & Pseudomonas spp.
(transient)
• Fungi: Candida albicans, dermatophytes

Staphylococcus aureus
• Coagulase positive, beta hemolytic on blood agar
• Key virulence factors
• Coagulase converts fibrinogen to fibrin, clots and
a fibrin shield inhibiting phagocytosis
• Capsule inhibits opsonization and phagocytosis,
protects from C’-medidated leukocyte destruction
• Protein A binds IgG1, IgG2, IgG4 Fc receptors, inhibits
opsonization, phagocytosis, leukocyte
chemoattractant, anticomplement
• Catalase prevents oxidative killing by immune cells
• Hyaluronidase, fibrinolysin, lipase, nuclease
• Penicillinase opens beta lactam ring inactivating
penicillin
• Cytolytic & exfoliative toxins, TSST, enterotoxin

Other Clinically Important Streptococci
• Streptococcus agalactiae
• Neonatal bloodstream infection and meningitis
• Post-partum sepsis (colonizes birth canal)
• Streptococcus pneumoniae
• Pneumonia, sinusitis, otitis media, meningitis, bloodstream
infection, sepsis
• Viridans streptococci
• Endocarditis, dental infections
--- SLIDE 14 ---
Pseudomonas aeruginosa
• Gram-negative, motile, oxidase-positive, non-lactose fermenting bacillus
• Colorless colonies on MacConkey agar
• Ubiquitous in moist environments
• Cell wall lipopolysaccharide (LPS) acts as an endotoxin resulting in sepsis
• Exotoxin A and esoenzyme S inhibit protein synthesis
• Infections of burns, surgical wounds, cystic fibrosis, ecthyma gangrenosum in
neutropenia
--- SLIDE 15 ---
Propionibacterium acnes
• Gram-positive, small,
anaerobic (aerotolerant)
• Colonizes skin, conjunctiva, external
ear, oropharynx
• Major cause of acne in young adults
• Combines with sebum to stimulate host
inflammatory response causing acne tissue damage

--- SLIDE 17 ---
Mycobacterium ulcerans: Buruli ulcer
• Acid fast bacillus related to M. tuberculosis
• Tropical Africa and Asia
• Associated with aquatic environments
• Large, erythematous ulcer
--- SLIDE 18 ---
Parvovirus B19 (Fifth Disease)
• Non-enveloped, icosahedral,
single-stranded, linear DNA virus
• Causes erythema infectiosum
• Childhood febrile illness
• Slapped-cheek rash

• Summary
1. The normal flora of the skin is predominantly Gram-positive
staphylococci and streptococci
2. Skin flora and pathogens can cause various infections of the skin as well
as toxin-mediated illness
3. Biochemical tests can help identify pathogens to direct therapy
