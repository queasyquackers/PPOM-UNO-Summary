--- SLIDE 4 ---
Human T-lymphotropic Virus
• HTLV-1: adult T-cell leukemia/lymphoma & HTLV-1 Associated Myelopathy
(Tropical Spastic Paraparesis)
• Trophic to mature T-cells & GLUT-1 receptor
• 5-20 million infected worldwide with clusters (Japan, Caribbean,
Central/South America, & Africa)
• ATLL: cutaneous lesions, lytic bone lesions, hypercalcemia
• Poor prognosis (5-yr survival <5%)
• Transmission:
a) Breastfeeding (important mode)
b) Transfusion
c) Contaminated needles
d) Sexual intercourse

Acute Retroviral Syndrome
• Abrupt onset of fever 2–4 weeks after
exposure.
• Accompanied by
➢ nonexudative pharyngitis and lymph-
adenitis,
➢ maculopapular skin rash on the head,
neck, and upper torso,
➢ headache and aseptic meningitis,
➢ anorexia, nausea, and diarrhea.
• The acute illness lasts 2–6 weeks. Lethargy
and fatigue can persist for several months.

--- SLIDE 59 ---
HIV: Summary
• HIV originated in Africa due to encroachment of forested land
exposed humans to SIV.
• HIV is transmitted via blood, sexual and prenatal exposures, all of
which can be prevented.
• The viral life cycle can be interrupted at many points by ART to
achieve an undetectable viral load.
• CD4 depletion results in progressive and eventually severe immune
suppression increasing the risk of infection and malignancy.
• The HIV pandemic has resulted in >40 million deaths and remains
active but there are many reasons to be hopeful that it will end
during out lifetime.
