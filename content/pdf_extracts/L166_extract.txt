Key Concepts
• Zoonosis: human disease derived from an animal reservoir
• e.g., Lyme disease from rodents, Trypanosoma from domesticated and wild
animals
• Vector: a living organism, typically an arthropod, that transmits an infection
from one host to another
• e.g, mosquito, tick
• Life cycle: the phases of growth and transmission that results in maturity and
reproduction, often requiring intermediate and definitive hosts
• e.g., Leishmania life cycle through rodents, sand flies and humans
• Infestation: the presence of parasites or ectoparasites on the body surface
• e.g., head lice (Pediculus humanus capitis) infestation

Bacteria: Bartonella
• B. henselae, B. quintana, B. bacilliformis
(many species cause human disease)
• Pleiomorphic, Gram-negative rod
• Catalase/oxidase negative
• Infect CD34+ cells (progenitors of
endothelial cells) and erythrocytes, followed
by dissemination to endovascular, lymphatic
and other organs
• Culture: slow growth (2-6 weeks)
• Stain: Warthin-Starry silver stain

Bartonella Skin Lesions
• Cat-scratch papule occurs at inoculation
site prior to regional lymphadenopathy
• Bacillary angiomatosis
• Bartonella henselae, B. quintana
• Host: immune suppressed, e.g., AIDS CD4+ <100
• Pathogenesis: capillary proliferation
• Diagnosis: biopsy of lesion shows neutrophilic
infiltrates, plump endothelial cells (epithelioid)
and basophilic aggregates of Bartonella organisms;
PCR; blood culture (prolonged incubation)
• Differential diagnosis: Kaposi’s sarcoma (also seen
in immune suppressed)
• Verruga peruana
• Chronic, eruptive skin phase of Carrion's disease (B. bacilliformis)
• Localized angioblastic hyperplasia (endothelial cell proliferation)
forming blood-filled vascular nodules (verrugas)

bird reservoir
• B. burgdorferi (Lyme disease)

• B. miyamotoi, B. recurrentis, B. hermsii,

--- SLIDE 10 ---
Lyme Disease Stages
• Stage 1: Early localized infection
• Erythema migrans (bullseye or target lesion)
• Occurs 3-30 days from site of tick bite
• Advancing Borrelia and lymphocyte/macrophage infiltration
• Stage 2: Early disseminated infection
• Occurs days to months after stage 1
• Transient bacteremia results in dissemination
• Intermittent rash, migratory arthralgias, headache, facial (Bell’s) palsy
• Carditis: AV block, myopericarditis
• Arthritis: non-symmetric, oligoarticular
• Stage 3: Late-stage infection
• Occurs months to years after stage 1
• Encephalopathy: altered mood, sleep, language; memory loss; brain fog
• Acrodermatitis chronica atrophicans (Europe)

Protozoa: Toxoplasma gondii
• Causes toxoplasmosis
• Obligate intracellular parasite that infects all warm-blooded animals
• Worldwide, more prevalent in warm climates
• ¼ of world’s population infected; 10% in U.S.
• Transmission from housecats, contaminated meat or shellfish
• Cats eat infected rodents and shed oocysts in stool
• Vertical transmission causes congenital toxoplasmosis: triad of chorioretinitis,
hydrocephalus, intracranial calcifications; less commonly blueberry muffin rash; prognosis variable to fatal
• Immune competent: usually asymptomatic, but may cause fever, lymphadenopathy, ocular disease; rarely myocarditis, hepatitis,
disseminated disease
• Reactvation disease in immune suppressed (AIDS CD4 <100/μL, A B C
SOT and HSCT) results in cerebral toxoplasmosis, chorioretinitis
and disseminated infection
• Diagnosis by serum antibody (elevated IgM in acute infection),
ring-enhancing lesions on brain CT/MRI (appears similar to
primary CNS lymphoma in AIDS)
• Treatment: pyrimethamine/sulfadiazine 4-6 weeks, clindamycin/
pyrimethamine, atovaquone/azithromycin, for CNS disease
trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole
Figure: A. tachyzoites, B. tissue cyst, C. ring-enhancing lesion on MRI
--- SLIDE 19 ---
Protozoa: Leishmania
• Central and S. America, Africa, Middle East
• Blood-borne, obligate intracellular parasite transmitted by
sandfly bite (>20 species infect humans)
• Sandfly infected via blood meal from rodents, dogs, foxes
• Adult sandfly injects human with promastigote stage
• Motile promastigotes (flagella) infect macrophages, transform
to amotile amastigotes (no flagella) which multiply
• Infection results in highly disfiguring
ulcers or fatal organ involvement
