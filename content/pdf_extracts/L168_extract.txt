Arenaviruses
• Enveloped, Spherical, -ss RNA, Segmented genome
• Rodents are natural reservoir
• Transmission from contaminated urine/feces from rodents
o Direct contact: Consumption, broken skin
o Inhalation of aerosolized particles
o Person-to-person: Contact with blood or excretions from contaminated
person
• Incubation period averages 10 -14 days
• Infect macrophages, induce interferon release → promote cell and vascular
damage
• Cell-induced immunopathologic effects significantly exacerbate tissue
destruction
• Lassa Virus, Machupo Virus, Junin Virus, Sabiá Virus, Guanarito Virus
Murray PR, et al., Medical Microbiology, 9th edition

Bunyaviruses
• Enveloped, Spherical, -ss RNA, Segmented Genome
• Transmission:
1. Most are arboviruses – transmitted by arthropods
– mosquitoes, ticks, flies
2. Hantavirus - aerosols from rodent urine and feces
• Initial viremia – flu-like illness
• Secondary viremia - virus may target specific tissues
some causing encephalitis, hepatic necrosis, or
hemorrhagic disease
• Hantavirus, Rift Valley Fever Virus, Nairo Virus

Hantavirus
• Symptoms develop 1-2 weeks after exposure
• Initially: Fever, chills, headache, nausea, abdominal pain, back
pain, redness of eyes, blurred vision
• May progress to hypotension, acute shock, vascular leakage and
acute kidney failure
• Hemorrhagic Fever with Renal Syndrome Patient with Hemorrhagic Fever with
Renal Syndrome displaying conjunctival
hemorrhage
• Treatment is mainly supportive
• Prognosis varies depending on serotype (mortality 1-15%)
• Prevention: rodent control -primary strategy. Avoid disturbing
rodent urine, droppings, saliva and nesting materials
Murray PR, et al., Medical Microbiology, 9th edition

Practice Question
A 28-year-old male presents with 5 days of temperature of 102F, severe headache,
retro-orbital pain, and myalgias described as 10/10 in severity. He recently returned
from a two-week vacation in Southeast Asia. Vitals are notable for a temp of 102 F, HR
100 BPM, RR 16, BP 110/70 mmHg. On physical exam, the patient is found to have a
diffuse maculopapular rash and mild petechiae on the trunk and lower extremities.
Laboratory results show thrombocytopenia.
1.
What is the etiological agent of this disease?
2.
The likely causative pathogen belongs to which viral family?
3.
What is the primary mode of transmission for this infection?
