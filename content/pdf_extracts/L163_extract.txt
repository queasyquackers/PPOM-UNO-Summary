Session Objectives
• Connect pathophysiology to clinical presentation, laboratory findings, treatment, and
management for various anemias of childhood, including iron def anemia, transient
erytheroblastopenia of childhood, Sickle Cell Anemia and its many complications
• Identify unique and shared characteristics in coagulation issues. Understand clinical presentation,
laboratory findings and treatment of Hemorrhagic Disease of the Newborn, Hemophilia and
Immune Thrombocytopenia.
• Explain the presentation, symptoms, and laboratory findings of pediatric Leukemias and general
approach to treatment.
• Compare and contrast etiology, clinical presentation and laboratory +/or radiographic findings of
Neuroblastoma and Wilms tumor, as well as Ewing Sarcoma and Osteosarcoma.
• Outline clinical presentation, diagnosis, and laboratory findings of Retinoblastoma
• Identify complications of pediatric oncology treatment, such as Myelosuppression and Tumor
Lysis syndrome.

Suspecting Anemia in Pediatrics
• Normal Hemoglobin (Hb) Values vary with age
• Clinical Features: (appear when Hb <8-9g/dL)
• Chronic:
• Usually Asymptomatic
• Mild/ moderate
• Pallor, weakness, fatigue, irritability

• Bone pain, hemolysis, jaundice, splenomegaly, lymphadenopathy

• Initial lab workup:
• CBC w/ differential, reticulocyte count, Ferritin

Transient Erythroblastopenia of Childhood
• Most common acquired red cell aplasia in children
• Transient suppression of RBC production → reticulocytopenia → normocytic anemia
Hb 6-8 g/dL but WBC and Plts are normal
Clinical presentation:
Previously healthy children between 6 mo - 3 yrs of age
Often following viral illness 2-3 months prior
Slowly developing pallor, slow recovery back to nl Hb
Treatment: none, self-limited,1-2 month complete recovery
NO OTHER RED FLAGS!!!
Red Flags: Chronic bone pain, HSM, other cell lines affected, petechiae, signs of hemolysis

--- SLIDE 10 ---
Thalassemia
- Inherited disorder of globin chain synthesis →
microcytic anemia
Types:
- Alpha Thalassemia: Deletion of α-globin genes
(1–4 genes)
- Beta Thalassemia: Mutations in β-globin genes
Clinical Spectrum:
- Minor (trait): Often asymptomatic, mild
microcytic anemia
- Intermedia: Moderate anemia, occasional
transfusions
- Major (Cooley's): Severe anemia, transfusion-
dependent, skeletal deformities
--- SLIDE 11 ---
Sickle Cell Anemia
• Genetics:
• Autosomal Recessive
• Point mutation: “GV6” Glutamine (Hydrophilic)→ Valine (Hydrophobic) 6th
residue in Hb β-chain results in HbS
• Non-polar → polar
• Pathophysiology
• Deoxygenated HbS interact and form rigid polymers → sickle shape
• Sickle shape leads to microvascular vaso-occlusion in postcapillary venules
• Diagnosis/Lab Findings:
• Newborn Screening
• Hemoglobin electrophoresis
• Blood smear: sickles, Howell jolly-bodies, target cells

Hemorrhagic Disease of the Newborn
A bleeding disorder in newborns caused by vitamin K deficiency,
leading to impaired coagulation. AKA Vit K deficient bleeding
Newborns have low vitamin K stores at birth.
Poor placental transfer, sterile gut (no vitamin K–producing flora), and immature liver
Other risk factor: infants of mothers on anticonvulsants or TB drugs.
mother on anticonvulsants (phenobarbital, phenytoin) interferes with Vit K fxn.
Prevented by receiving Vitamin K injection at birth (standard of care) –life saving prophylaxis
Classic: Week 1- bleeding from umbilicus, GI tract, circumcision.
Later onset: 2–12 weeks — may present with intracranial hemorrhage
Labs: Prolonged PT, normal platelets and PTT

--- SLIDE 31 ---
Von Willebrand Disease
• Most common inherited bleeding disorder (genetics: most AD)
• Caused by a defect in VWF (aids in platelet adhesions and protecting factor VIII)
• Many different types; may be a quantitative or qualitative defect
• Most common is type 1: mild to moderate quantitative defect
• Clinical presentation
• Mucocutaneous bleeding (epistaxis, bleeding gums)
• Postoperative bleedings (eg after a dental extraction)
• Adolescents: excessive menstrual bleeding
• Diagnosis: VWF antigen, ristocetin cofactor activity, factor VIII level, screening test not always
reliable (may need to rpt)
• Lab values: Normal PT, ~ increased PTT (depending on FVIII levels), increased bleeding
time
• Treatment: Desmopressin (DDAVP), Fresh frozen plasma

--- SLIDE 36 ---
Suspecting Leukemia
• Early—nonspecific
• Anorexia (unintentional weight loss), fatigue, malaise, irritability, and
intermittent low-grade fever.
• Chronic Bone/joint pain, limp- (don’t want to play/ normal activities red flag)
• Late—bone marrow failure
• Pallor, fatigue, exercise intolerance--anemia
• Petechiae, ecchymoses, oral mucosal bleeding or epistaxis--thrombocytopenia
• Persistent fever (infections)--neutropenia
• Organ Infiltration
• Lymphadenopathy, Hepatosplenomegaly, Testicular enlargement
• Central nervous system (CNS) involvement (headache, seizures)
• “Blueberry muffin” appearance in AML, DIC*
--- SLIDE 37 ---
Acute Leukemia Diagnosis
• CBC w/ diff and peripheral smear
• Anemia
• Thrombocytopenia
• WBC can vary- very low or very high
• Blasts
• Bone marrow aspirate and biopsy
• >25% blast cells
• Also evaluate CSF with LP. Poorer prognosis w/ CNS involvement
• Determining specific type/subtype
• Flow Cytometry—cell surface markers
• Cytogenetics
• Molecular studies
• t(9;22) Philadelphia chromosome (CML)
• AML—Auer Rods, MPO+
• ALL—terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase TdT+
--- SLIDE 38 ---
Acute Leukemia Treatment
• Chemotherapy
• Risk stratification
• Age, WBC at diagnosis, immunophenotype and cytogenetics, response to
initial treatment
• Intrathecal for CNS
• Presence of genetic disorders (Down syndrome) can influence treatment
• Relapses
• Cranial or craniospinal irradiation in later phases if high risk of CNS relapse
• Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplant (HSCT) for patients with relapse- improves
long term survival
• Watch For:
• Tumor Lysis Syndrome
• Myelosuppression and risk for infection
