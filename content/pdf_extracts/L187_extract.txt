Cutaneous Rheumatology
• There are a number systemic diseases that can affect the
skin, hair, and nails, many are AUTOIMMUNE w/ tissue
destruction in numerous organ systems

– There are characteristic changes of the connective

conditions

--- SLIDE 5 ---
Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)
:
multisystem autoimmune disorder involving connective tissue & blood vessels.
DX SLE: 4/11 of RASH OR PAIN
1. Malar/Butterfly Rash - erythematous plaques, spares nasolabial folds
2. Discoid Rash - erythematous papules with adherent keratotic scaling and
follicular plugging; atrophic scarring may occur with chronic lesions
3. Arthritis - pain in 2 or more joints
4. Serositis - fluid around the heart or lung
5. Hematologic disorder - hemolytic anemia (RBC), leukopenia < 4000/uL
(WBC), lymphopenia < 1500/uL , thrombocytopenia< 100,000/uL (Platelets)
6. Oral ulcers - painless
7. Renal disease - proteinuria (protein in urine)
8. Photosensitivity
9. Antinuclear antibody (ANA): >95%
10. Immunologic disorder: autoantibodies (next slide)
11. Neurologic disorder - seizures or psychosis
--- SLIDE 6 ---
Autoantibodies in SLE
• anti-dsDNA (can indicate kidney and skin disease)
• anti-Smith (kidney disease)
• anti-nucleosomes (kidney and skin disease)
• anti-Ro/SS-A (skin and kidney disease, fetal heart problems)
• anti-La/SS-B (fetal heart problems)
• anti-histone (drug-induced lupus most commonly caused by
“HIP” - Hydralazine, Isoniazid, and Procainamide)
• anti-phospholipid (thrombosis, pregnancy loss)
• anti-NMDA receptor (brain disease)
• anti-α-actinin (kidney disease)
• anti-C1q (kidney disease)
• VDRL (false positive for syphilis)

--- SLIDE 11 ---
Evaluation & Tx of Patients With Lupus
• History and Physical
• Bloodwork: CBC, autoantibodies
• Urinalysis - proteinuria, hematuria
• Biopsy - lesional, non-lesional, and non-sunexposed
– Immunofluorescence (lupus band test)
• Positive in lesional skin 90%
• Normal sun exposed 75%
• Tx:
– Sunscreens
– Steroids (Topical, Intralesional, Systemic)
– anti-malarials (i.e. hydroxycholorquine)
– Immunosuppressants

Systemic Manifestations
• AM stiffness of joints
• Esophageal dysphagia - difficulty swallowing
• Lung disease - associated with anti-Jo-1
• Heart disease - cardiomyopathy
• Malignancies - mc. ovarian cancer in females.
--- SLIDE 16 ---
Evaluation & Tx of Dermatomyositis
• History - malignancy, infections, drugs
• Physical and testing
– Female: pelvic (pap smear), breast exam (mammo)
– Male: rectal exam
– Both-chest x-ray, stool guiac (test blood in stool)
• Evaluation of muscle disease: muscle biopsy, EMG
PFT’s (pulmonary fxn tests), echocardiogram
Esophageal studies-muscle weakness
• Labs
- Creatine phosphokinase (CPK - most specific for muscle disease)
- Antibody studies (anti-Jo-1)
Tx: Physical therapy (helps muscles), prednisone (systemic steroids),
immunosuppressants
--- SLIDE 17 ---
Scleroderma - “sclerosis of skin”
• Multi-system autoimmune disease
• Fibroblasts overproduce collagen → fibrosis
• Sclerotic changes of skin and internal organs
(lungs, heart, GI)
• 2 Categories:
– Localized: morphea
– Systemic:
• limited (CREST vs acrosclerosis)
• progressive (multi system)
--- SLIDE 18 ---
Localized Scleroderma - Morphea
• Violaceous to ivory hardened skin, with possible
underlying atrophy (depression)
• It may be:
– Solitary
– Generalized
– Linear
– “En coup de sabre” - linear morphea of the head
– Pansclerotic: involvement of dermis, fat, muscle,
bone (disabling)

--- SLIDE 22 ---
Systemic Scleroderma
Limited Type: CREST
- Calcinosis: calcium deposits in skin, anti-Centromere ab
- Raynaud’s
- Esophageal dysfunction
- Sclerodactyly (thick/tight tapered skin of the fingers and toes)
- Telangiectasias (hands, face)
--- SLIDE 23 ---
Systemic Scleroderma
Progressive Type (PSS): skin & internal organs
– Raynaud’s (1st manifestations of PSS)
– Pulmonary fibrosis
– Kidney failure
– Heart attack and failure
– “mask like facies”, loss of facial lines

--- SLIDE 25 ---
Sarcoidosis
• Systemic granulomatous disease of unknown etiology; more
common in African Americans
• Primarily affects the lungs - bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy
• Skin - granulomatous lesions, lupus pernio, brown-purple papules
– lesions are diascopy positive: blanching with a glass slide
reveals “apple-jelly” color in the lesions
– Erythema nodosum is the most common non-specific lesion in
the skin (good prognosis)
• non-caseating granulomas
• hypercalcemia
• elevated angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE)

--- SLIDE 30 ---
Kawasaki Disease aka
Mucocutaneous Lymph Node Syndrome
5/6 Criteria: CRASH & Burn
- Conjunctival Injection
- Rash: erythematous, spreads from palms/soles to trunk
- Adenopathy, lymph node mass > 1.5cm
- Strawberry tongue, oral fissures, lip crusting
- Hand/foot edema, erythema, induration, desquamation of digits
- Burn: unexplained fever > 5days
Affects children < 10yo, 2% mortality: ruptured coronary aneurysm
Tx: salicylates, IVIG

Review & Mnemonics
• Lupus Erythematosis: RASH OR PAIN
• Dermatomyositis: Heliotrope rash & Grotton’s papules
• Kawasaki Disease: CRASH & Burn
• Behcet’s Disease: recurrent oral & genital lesions, uveitis
• Sarcoidosis: Lung infiltrates & apple jelly skin
• Scleroderma: Localized vs Systemic
• Human Graft vs Host Disease: attacking foreign tissue, acute vs chronic
• HHT/OWR: nose and GI bleed
• PJS: GI polyps lip spots
• Cowdens/MHS: multiple tumors, facial warts
• Gardner: bumpy face and jaw with too many teeth, GI polyps
• Acrochordons: skin tags associated w/ DM, obesity, chronic G polyps
• Cirrhosis of liver: jaundice, spider telangiectasias, palmar erythema, terry nails
• Pancreatic cancer: Sister Mary Joseph nodule
• CV: relapsing polychondritis – painful cartilage
• Chronic renal failure: anemia, xerosis, pruritis, uremic frost, calcification
• Dermatitis Herpetiformis (Celiac Dx / Gluten): pruritus, grouped papules/vesicles on extensors
• Kwashiorkor: edematous malnutrition with flag sign and enamel paint
• Acrodermatitis enteropathica: baby w/ zinc deficiency, vesculobullous and erythematous,
diarrhea, alopecia, frequent infections. may resemble atopic dermatitis
• Niacin deficiency / Pellagra – 3D’s: dermatitis (casal’s necklace), diarrhea, dementia
• Scurvy: Vit C deficiency due to Collagen defect, follicular hyperkeratotic plaques, corkscrew
hairs, gingivitis, anemia
