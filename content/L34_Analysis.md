```text
Key points:
1. SNS physiology → Mechanisms → direct-acting pharmacologic effects:
The synthetic direct-acting agonists in Part 2 of this presentation bind directly to adrenergic 
receptors β2, β3, and α2, receptors. They are widely used in clinical practice.
2. SNS physiology → Mechanisms → indirect-acting and mixed-acting pharmacologic effects
Indirect-acting adrenergic agonists cross the blood-brain barrier and cause the release, inhibit the 
reuptake, or inhibit the degradation of biogenic amines. They potentiate the effects of endogenous 
epinephrine or norepinephrine but do not directly bind to or affect postsynaptic receptors.
Mixed-acting drugs act centrally and peripherally to cause the release of NE and EPI from storage 
vesicles and direct action on  and  adrenergic receptors.
3. Pharmacologic effects → therapeutic uses (next slide)
Direct-acting sympathomimetics have various therapeutic uses.
Indirect-acting sympathomimetics are used (and abused) for their sympathomimetic effects in the 
CNS.
Mixed-acting agents, ephedrine and pseudoephedrine, have limited use.
4. Actions → adverse effects and cautions
Adverse effects and cautions are typical of the sympathomimetics class. 6
```

### Slide 7 (Score: 280.00)
```text
• Beta-2 agonists induce bronchial smooth muscle relaxation and bronchodilation. They are 
administered primarily by oral inhalation for direct action on lung tissue in the management of 
asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).
• Beta-3 agonists relax urinary bladder smooth muscle for the treatment of overactive bladder.
• Centrally-acting alpha-2 agonists act by reducing sympathetic outflow from the CNS. Thus, they 
are useful in the management of hypertension. Clonidine is also used for reducing withdrawal 
symptoms related to substance abuse. Methyldopa is used primarily for management of chronic 
hypertension in pregnancy.
• Indirect-acting sympathomimetics, amphetamines, MAO inhibitors, and catecholamine reuptake 
inhibitors increase synaptic levels of norepinephrine. Therapeutic uses are attention deficit 
disorder and depression.
• Mixed-acting sympathomimetics (eg, ephedrine) cause the release of norepinephrine from 
storage vesicles and act directly on adrenergic receptors. Ephedrine is used parenterally for 
anesthesia-induced hypotension and orally as a bronchodilator. Pseudoephedrine is less potent 
and taken orally for nasal decongestion.
```

```text
• Indirect-acting
• Mixed-acting
```

```text
• Beta-2 receptor agonists are inhaled bronchodilators in the management of asthma and COPD.

• Amphetamines promote the release of catecholamines from storage vesicles (predominant

catecholamines by competitive inhibition at the transporters. These drugs are used in the 
management of attention deficit disorder. They readily enter the CNS and have marked stimulant 
effects on mood and alertness and depressant effect on appetite. Amphetamines have a high 
potential for abuse.
```
