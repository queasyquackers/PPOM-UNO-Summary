## Introduction and Objectives
This lecture serves as a preparation for the OMM Lab on Sacral Muscle Energy Treatment (MET), specifically focusing on **Unilateral** and **Bilateral** sacral dysfunctions. It builds upon previous sacral mechanics concepts.

The core goal is to differentiate between **Physiologic** (Flexion) and **Non-Physiologic** (Extension) dysfunctions using static landmarks (Sulci, ILAs) and dynamic testing (Spring, Sphinx).

---

## Sacral Diagnosis Review (Slide 2-3)

### Physiologic Motion (Nutated)
*   **Sacral Flexion:** The sacral base moves anteriorly.
*   **Respiratory:** Occurs during **Inhalation**.
*   **Associated Dysfunctions:** Unilateral Flexion, Bilateral Flexion, Forward Torsions.

### Non-Physiologic Motion (Counternutated)
*   **Sacral Extension:** The sacral base moves posteriorly.
*   **Respiratory:** Occurs during **Exhalation**.
*   **Associated Dysfunctions:** Unilateral Extension, Bilateral Extension, Backward Torsions.

---

## Unilateral Sacral Dysfunctions (Slide 4-6)

These dysfunctions occur when one side of the sacrum moves more than the other, but without a defined oblique axis like torsions. They are often termed **Shears**.

### 1. Unilateral Sacral Flexion (Inferior Shear)
*   **Mechanism:** One side of the sacral base moves anteriorly and gets stuck deep.
*   **Findings (e.g., Left Unilateral Flexion):**
    *   **Seated Flexion Test:** Positive on the **Dysfunctional Side** (Left).
    *   **Sacral Sulcus:** **Deep** on the dysfunctional side (Left).
    *   **ILA:** **Posterior/Inferior** on the dysfunctional side (Left).
    *   **L5:** Rotated **Toward** the dysfunctional side (Deep sulcus attracts L5).
*   **Provocation Tests:**
    *   **Spring Test:** **Negative** (Good spring / "Good Dog").
    *   **Sphinx Test:** **Negative** (Symmetry improves or stays same).

### 2. Unilateral Sacral Extension (Superior Shear)
*   **Mechanism:** One side of the sacral base moves posteriorly and gets stuck shallow.
*   **Findings (e.g., Left Unilateral Extension):**
    *   **Seated Flexion Test:** Positive on the **Dysfunctional Side** (Left).
    *   **Sacral Sulcus:** **Shallow** on the dysfunctional side (Left).
    *   **ILA:** **Anterior/Superior** on the dysfunctional side (Left).
    *   **L5:** Rotated **Away** from the dysfunctional side (Shallow sulcus pushes L5 away).
*   **Provocation Tests:**
    *   **Spring Test:** **Positive** (Resistance / "Bad Dog").
    *   **Sphinx Test:** **Positive** (Asymmetry worsens).

:::pearl
**Shear Rule:**
In a Unilateral Dysfunction (Shear), the **Deep Sulcus** and **Posterior ILA** are on the **SAME SIDE**.
*(Contrast with Torsions, where they are on OPPOSITE sides).*
:::

## Bilateral Sacral Dysfunctions (Slide 8-11)

These involve the entire sacral base moving together (Symmetric).

### 1. Bilateral Sacral Flexion
*   **Mechanism:** Both sacral bases move anteriorly (Nutated) and get stuck. common in post-partum or after heavy lifting.
*   **Findings:**
    *   **Seated Flexion Test:** False Negative (Symmetric).
    *   **Sacral Sulci:** **Deep** Bilaterally.
    *   **ILAs:** **Posterior/Inferior** Bilaterally.
*   **Provocation Tests:**
    *   **Spring Test:** **Negative**.
    *   **Sphinx Test:** **Negative**.

### 2. Bilateral Sacral Extension
*   **Mechanism:** Both sacral bases move posteriorly (Counternutated). "Extension = Exhalation = Excavation" (shallow).
*   **Findings:**
    *   **Seated Flexion Test:** False Negative (Symmetric).
    *   **Sacral Sulci:** **Shallow** Bilaterally.
    *   **ILAs:** **Anterior/Superior** Bilaterally.
*   **Provocation Tests:**
    *   **Spring Test:** **Positive**.
    *   **Sphinx Test:** **Positive**.

---

## Summary of Diagnosis (Slide 13)

| Dysfunction | Sulcus | ILA | Spring Test |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Unilateral Flexion** | Deep (Same side) | Posterior (Same side) | Negative (-) |
| **Unilateral Extension** | Shallow (Same side) | Anterior (Same side) | Positive (+) |
| **Bilateral Flexion** | Deep (Bilateral) | Posterior (Bilateral) | Negative (-) |
| **Bilateral Extension** | Shallow (Bilateral) | Anterior (Bilateral) | Positive (+) |

:::highyield
**Spring & Sphinx Rules**
*   **Spring Test:** Tests for **Extension** (Resistance).
    *   Positive = Stuck in Extension ("Bad Dog" - bites/hard).
    *   Negative = Flexion allowed ("Good Dog" - soft/springy).
*   **Sphinx Test:** Tests for **Flexion** (Ability to move forward).
    *   Positive (Worsens) = Cannot flex (Stuck in Extension).
    *   Negative (Improves) = Can flex (Stuck in Flexion or Normal).
:::

:::correlate
**L5 Rules (Fryette's)**
*   **Rotation:** L5 rotates **Opposite** to the Sacrum.
    *   Sacrum facing Left -> L5 rotates Right.
*   **Sidebending:** L5 sidebends **Same side** as the Oblique Axis (for torsions).
    *   For shears (Unilateral), L5 simply follows the distinct "Deep Sulcus attracts, Shallow Repels" rule.
:::

## Review Questions

**1. A patient presents with low back pain. Structural exam reveals a positive Seated Flexion Test on the Right. The Right Sacral Sulcus is deep, and the Right ILA is posterior/inferior. The Spring test is negative. What is the diagnosis?**

A. Right Unilateral Sacral Extension
B. Left Unilateral Sacral Extension
C. Right Unilateral Sacral Flexion
D. Left Unilateral Sacral Flexion
E. Right on Right Sacral Torsion

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: C (Right Unilateral Sacral Flexion)**

**Rationale**:
The positive Seated Flexion Test lateralizes to the **Right**.
Deep Sulcus + Posterior ILA on the **SAME side** indicates a **Unilateral (Shear)** dysfunction, not a torsion.
Since the landmarks (Deep/Posterior) indicate the sacral base has moved anteriorly (Flexion), and the Spring test is Negative (confirms Flexion), it is a Right Unilateral Sacral Flexion.

</details>

**2. In a patient with a Left Unilateral Sacral Extension, what are the expected findings for the L5 vertebra?**

A. Rotated Left
B. Rotated Right
C. Neutral
D. Flexed
E. Sidebent Right

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: A (Rotated Left)**

**Rationale**:
In Left Unilateral Extension, the Left sacral base is **Posterior** (Shallow). A shallow/posterior base "pushes" the L5 transverse process away (or conversely, the deep right base attracts it). Wait, let's re-verify: L5 rotates **opposite** sacrum.
If Left Sacrum is Extended (Posterior), it is facing **Right** (Left side back = facing Right). Therefore L5 should rotate **Left**.
Alternatively: The Left base is posterior/shallow. The Right base is anterior/deep relative to it. L5 rotates toward the **Deep** sulcus (Right).
Let's check the text rule: "L5 Dx. (F/E)RxSx".
Reference Slide 6 (Left Univ Extension): L5 Dx is listed.
Biomechanics: Deep Sulcus attracts L5. In Left Unilateral Extension, the **Right** sulcus is deeper (relatively) because the Left is shallow/posterior. So L5 rotates **Right**.
Let's correct the answer to **B**.

**Correction:**
In Left Uni. Ext -> Left Base Posterior -> Sacrum looks Left? No, Back of sacrum on left is posterior. Front of sacrum faces **Right**.
Rule: L5 rotates Opposite Sacrum. Sacrum faces Right -> L5 rotates **Left**.
Contradiction check:
Deep sulcus rule: L5 rotates toward deep sulcus. In Left Uni Ext, Left is Shallow. Right is Deep. L5 rotates Toward Right.
So L5 Rot Right.
Let's stick to the Deep Sulcus rule as it is most robust for Shears.
**Answer: B (Rotated Right)**.

</details>

**3. Which of the following findings is consistent with a Bilateral Sacral Flexion?**

A. Positive Spring Test
B. Bilateral Shallow Sulci
C. Bilateral Posterior/Inferior ILAs
D. Positive Sphinx Test
E. Bilateral Anterior/Superior ILAs

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: C (Bilateral Posterior/Inferior ILAs)**

**Rationale**:
Bilateral Flexion = Nutated (Base Forward, Apex Back).
Apex Back = Posterior ILAs.
Base Forward = Deep Sulci.
Spring Test should be Negative (Good Spring).

</details>

**4. The Spring Test applies a force in which direction?**

A. Anteriorly on the Sacral Base
B. Posteriorly on the Sacral Apex
C. Anteriorly on the L5 Spinous Process
D. Superiorly on the Ischial Tuberosities
E. Medially on the PSIS

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: A (Anteriorly on the Sacral Base)**

**Rationale**:
The Spring test involves applying a PA (Posterior-to-Anterior) force on the **lumbosacral junction/sacral base** to assess for resistance (extension) or compliance (flexion).

</details>

**5. Which axis is associated with Unilateral Sacral Dysfunctions (Shears)?**

A. Superior Transverse
B. Middle Transverse
C. Inferior Transverse
D. Oblique Axis
E. No Axis (Non-physiologic shift)

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: E (No Axis)**

**Rationale**:
Unilateral dysfunctions are often called **Shears** because they do not occur around a specific axis (unlike torsions which use Oblique, or Nutation/Counternutation which use Middle Transverse). They are a shift of one side. NOTE: Some texts ascribe undefined axes, but "No Transverse/Oblique Axis" is the standard teaching for distinguishing them from torsions.

</details>

## Mind Map Structure

### Sacral Dysfunctions (Part 2)

#### Unilateral (Shears)
*   **Unilateral Flexion (Inferior)**
    *   **Sulcus**: Deep (Same side)
    *   **ILA**: Posterior (Same side)
    *   **Tests**: Spring (-), Sphinx (-)
*   **Unilateral Extension (Superior)**
    *   **Sulcus**: Shallow (Same side)
    *   **ILA**: Anterior (Same side)
    *   **Tests**: Spring (+), Sphinx (+)

#### Bilateral (Symmetric)
*   **Bilateral Flexion**
    *   **Sulci**: Deep Bilaterally
    *   **ILAs**: Posterior Bilaterally
    *   **Tests**: Spring (-)
*   **Bilateral Extension**
    *   **Sulci**: Shallow Bilaterally
    *   **ILAs**: Anterior Bilaterally
    *   **Tests**: Spring (+)
