## 1. Parkinson's Disease (PD) Pharmacology
**Goal:** Restore Dopamine (DA) levels or reduce Acetylcholine (ACh) activity to fix the DA/ACh imbalance.

### 1.1 Levodopa / Carbidopa (Sinemet)
*   **Mechanism:**
    *   **Levodopa (L-Dopa):** Precursor to Dopamine. Crosses the Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB) via neutral amino acid transporter. Converted to DA by **Dopa Decarboxylase (DDC)**.
    *   **Carbidopa:** Inhibits **Peripheral DDC**. Does **NOT** cross BBB.
    *   **Why Combine?** Without Carbidopa, only 1-3% of L-Dopa enters the brain (most converted peripherally $\rightarrow$ side effects). With Carbidopa, **10%** enters brain.
*   **Adverse Effects:**
    *   **Peripheral:** Nausea/Vomiting (CTZ stimulation), **Orthostatic Hypotension**, Cardiac Arrhythmias.
    *   **Central:** **Dyskinesias** (writhing movements after long-term use), **Psychosis** (Hallucinations/Delusions).
*   **Drug Interactions:**
    *   **High Protein Meals:** Amino acids compete for transporter $\rightarrow$ Decrease efficacy.
    *   **Vitamin B6 (Pyridoxine):** Increases peripheral breakdown of L-Dopa (Avoid high doses).

### 1.2 COMT Inhibitors ("-capone")
*   **Mechanism:** Inhibit Catechol-O-Methyltransferase (COMT), blocking conversion of L-Dopa to 3-O-Methyldopa (3-OMD). **Prolongs L-Dopa half-life** ("On" time).
*   **Agents:**
    *   **Entacapone:** Peripheral inhibition only.
    *   **Tolcapone:** Central & Peripheral. **Black Box Warning: Hepatotoxicity** (Monitor LFTs).
    *   **Opicapone:** Long-acting (Once daily).
*   **Side Effect:** **Orange/Brown discoloration of urine**.

### 1.3 MAO-B Inhibitors ("-giline")
*   **Mechanism:** Inhibit Monoamine Oxidase B (MAO-B), preventing central breakdown of Dopamine.
*   **Agents:**
    *   **Selegiline:** Metabolized to **Amphetamine/Methamphetamine** $\rightarrow$ **Insomnia** (Do not take late at night).
    *   **Rasagiline:** Irreversible inhibitor.
    *   **Safinamide:** Reversible MAO-B inhibitor + Glutamate modulation.
*   **Neuroprotection:** May prevent MPTP conversion to toxic MPP+ (preventing mitochondrial damage).
*   **Interactions:** Risk of **Serotonin Syndrome** if combined with SSRIs/TCAs (though risk is lower with selective MAO-B vs MAO-A).

### 1.4 Dopamine Agonists
*   **Mechanism:** Directly stimulate Post-synaptic Dopamine Receptors (D2/D3). No conversion needed.
*   **Agents:**
    *   **Pramipexole (Mirapex):** D3 > D2. Antioxidant properties. Also for **Restless Leg Syndrome (RLS)**.
    *   **Ropinirole (Requip):** D2/D3. Also for RLS.
    *   **Rotigotine:** Transdermal Patch (stable levels).
*   **Adverse Effects:**
    *   **Impulse Control Disorders:** Pathological Gambling, Hypersexuality, Shopping.
    *   **Sleep Attacks:** Sudden onset of sleep without warning.

### 1.5 Rescue Therapy: Apomorphine
*   **Use:** Acute **subcutaneous** injection for severe "Off" episodes (Freezing).
*   **Adverse Effect:** Severe Nausea/Vomiting.
*   **Management:** Pre-treat with **Trimethobenzamide**.
*   **Contraindication:** **Do NOT use 5-HT3 Antagonists (Ondansetron/Zofran)** $\rightarrow$ Causes profound **Hypotension** and Syncope.

### 1.6 Amantadine
*   **Mechanism:** Antiviral (Flu A - M2 channel blocker). In PD: **NMDA Receptor Antagonist** + increases DA release.
*   **Use:** Treating **Levodopa-induced Dyskinesias**.
*   **Side Effect:** **Livedo Reticularis** (Purplish, net-like skin rash).

### 1.7 Adenosine Antagonist: Istradefylline
*   **Mechanism:** Adenosine A2A receptor antagonist in Basal Ganglia. Reduces "Off" time.
*   **Side Effects:** Insomnia (Structurally related to **Caffeine**).

### 1.8 Anticholinergics (Benztropine, Trihexyphenidyl)
*   **Use:** Tremor predominant PD.
*   **Adverse Effect:** Antimuscarinic (Dry mouth, urinary retention, confusion). **Avoid in Geriatrics (Beers List)**.

## 2. Alzheimer's Disease (AD) Pharmacology
**Goal:** Increase Acetylcholine (ACh) or reduce Glutamate Excitotoxicity.

### 2.1 Acetylcholinesterase Inhibitors (AChEIs)
*   **Mechanism:** Inhibit breakdown of ACh in the synaptic cleft.
*   **Agents:**
    *   **Donepezil (Aricept).**
    *   **Rivastigmine:** Inhibits AChE and Butyrylcholinesterase. Patch available.
    *   **Galantamine:** Also acts as a nicotinic receptor agonist.
*   **Adverse Effects:** **Cholinergic Excess (SLUDGE)**.
    *   Nausea, Diarrhea, Vomiting.
    *   **Bradycardia:** Risk of syncope (Caution with Beta-blockers).
    *   Bronchoconstriction (Caution in asthma/COPD).

### 2.2 NMDA Receptor Antagonist
*   **Agent:** **Memantine**.
*   **Mechanism:** **Uncompetitive NMDA Antagonist**. Blocks tonic/excessive glutamate stimulation (excitotoxicity) while allowing physiologic signals.
*   **Use:** Moderate to Severe AD. Can be combined with AChEIs.

### 2.3 Anti-Amyloid Monoclonal Antibodies
*   **Mechanism:** Bind and clear Amyloid Beta plaques.
*   **Agents:**
    *   **Lecanemab:** Targets Protofibrils.
    *   **Donanemab.**
*   **Adverse Effects:** **ARIA (Amyloid-Related Imaging Abnormalities)**.
    *   **ARIA-E:** Edema/Effusion.
    *   **ARIA-H:** Micro-Hemorrhage/Hemosiderin.
    *   **Monitoring:** Requires serial **MRIs**.
