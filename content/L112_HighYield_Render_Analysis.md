### Slide 30 (Score: 141.00)
```text
• Etiology
• IV disc degeneration that begins with decreased NP water & proteoglycan content → impairs load-bearing ability
• Type II collagen (weaker) replaces type I →  tears/fissures develop in the AF. 
• Vertebral endplates calcify, impairing nutrient diffusion to the intervertebral disc
• Progresses to Disc bulging, herniation, facet joint hypertrophy, thickening of ligamentum flavum
• Location
• Of all symptomatic intervertebral disc herniations, less than 4% occur in the thoracic spine
• Lumbar herniated NP (HNP); most common at L4 -L5, then L5-S1
• Usually posterolateral (Posterior longitudinal ligament is the weakest)
• If disc impinges nerve roots it causes lumbar radiculopathy 
• Ie. Sciatica if it is one of roots that contributes to the sciatic nerve (L4-S3)
Lumbar Herniated Disc (Herniated Nucleus 
Pulposus; HNP)
Sources: Miller Essential Orthopaedics, 2020. Chapter 113
Moore Clinically Oriented Anatomy, 7th ed
```

```text
• Below this level spinal canal filled with L2-S4 nerve 
roots
• Free floating nerve roots look like a horse’s tail & thus called
```
