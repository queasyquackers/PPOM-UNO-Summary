## 1. Broad Spectrum Agents
**Story Mode:**
Broad-spectrum anti-seizure drugs (ASDs) are the "utility players" of epilepsy treatment. Because they target multiple mechanisms—often hitting sodium channels to stop firing *and* enhancing GABA to increase inhibition—they are effective against a wide variety of seizure types, including focal, generalized tonic-clonic, and absence seizures. This makes them an excellent choice when the exact seizure type is unclear.

### 1.1 Valproate (Divalproex)
*   **Mechanisms:** The definition of "Broad Spectrum."
    *   Blocks voltage-dependent **Sodium ($Na^+$) channels**.
    *   Blocks T-type **Calcium ($Ca^{2+}$) channels** (Treats Absence).
    *   Enhances **GABA** activity (inhibitory).
*   **Therapeutic Uses:**
    *   **Seizure:** First-line for many types: Focal, Generalized, Absence, Myoclonic. Used for Status Epilepticus (off-label).
    *   **Non-Seizure:** **Bipolar Disorder** (Acute Mania) and **Migraine Prophylaxis**.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   Mitochondrial metabolism (Beta-oxidation).
    *   **Inhibitor** of CYP enzymes (increases levels of other drugs like Lamotrigine).
*   **Adverse Effects:**
    *   **GI:** Nausea/Vomiting (common, limit by taking with food or ER formulations).
    *   **Cosmetic:** **Weight Gain**, **Hair Loss (Alopecia)**, Tremor.
    *   **Serious:** **Hepatotoxicity** (Monitor LFTs), **Pancreatitis**, **Thrombocytopenia**.
    *   **Teratogenicity:** **Neural Tube Defects** (Spina Bifida). Avoid in pregnancy if possible.

:::correlate
**Hyperammonemic Encephalopathy**
**The Mechanism:**
Valproate is metabolized in the mitochondria and can interfere with the Urea Cycle.
*   **Result:** Accumulation of Ammonia ($NH_3$) in the blood.
*   **Presentation:** Acute change in mental status (confusion, lethargy) in a patient taking Valproate, even with normal liver enzymes.
*   **Risk Factor:** Concurrent use with **Topiramate** (synergistic risk).
:::

### 1.2 Topiramate (Topamax)
*   **Mechanisms:**
    *   Blocks $Na^+$ channels.
    *   Enhances GABA-A activity.
    *   Antagonizes AMPA/Kainate (Glutamate) receptors.
    *   **Weak Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitor**.
*   **Therapeutic Uses:**
    *   **Seizure:** Focal, Generalized, Lennox-Gastaut Syndrome.
    *   **Non-Seizure:** **Migraine Prophylaxis**, Weight Loss (in combination with Phentermine).
*   **Adverse Effects:**
    *   **CNS:** "Dope-a-max" – Cognitive slowing, word-finding difficulties, sedation, paresthesias (tingling).
    *   **Weight Loss** (contrast with Valproate).
    *   **Renal:** **Kidney Stones** (Calcium Phosphate stones due to alkaline urine from carbonic anhydrase inhibition).
    *   **Ocular:** Acute Myopia/Angle Closure Glaucoma.

:::correlate
**Hypohidrosis & Hyperthermia**
**The Mechanism:**
Topiramate's carbonic anhydrase inhibition can impair sweat gland function.
*   **Target:** **Children** are most vulnerable.
*   **Risk:** Reduced sweating ($\downarrow$ heat dissipation) leads to dangerous overheating (**Hyperthermia**) during play or hot weather.
*   **Management:**Counsel parents to monitor for lack of sweating during exertion.
:::

### 1.3 Levetiracetam (Keppra)
*   **Mechanism:** Unique. Binds to **SV2A** (Synaptic Vesicle Protein 2A).
    *   Prevents vesicle docking and neurotransmitter release.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Excellent Profile:** 100% Bioavailability, Renal excretion (unchanged), **No drug-drug interactions**.
*   **Adverse Effects:**
    *   Generally well-tolerated (sedation, dizziness).
    *   **Neuropsychiatric:** **"Keppra Rage"** – Irritability, agitation, aggression, or psychosis (rare).

## 2. Narrow Spectrum Agents
**Story Mode:**
Unlike the broad-spectrum agents, these drugs have a specific lane. Ethosuximide is a sniper: it hits one target (T-type channels) to fix one problem (Absence seizures). Gabapentinoids are "structural mimics" of GABA that don't actually touch GABA receptors but block calcium channels to stop neuropathic pain and focal seizures.

### 2.1 Ethosuximide
*   **Mechanism:** Blocks **T-Type Calcium Channels** in the Thalamus.
    *   disrupts the 3Hz spike-and-wave rhythm of absence seizures.
*   **Therapeutic Use:** **Absence Seizures ONLY** (Childhood Absence Epilepsy).
    *   *Note:* Ineffective for tonic-clonic seizures.
*   **Adverse Effects:**
    *   **GI:** Nausea/Vomiting/Pain (very common).
    *   **CNS:** Fatigue, Dizziness.
    *   **Rare:** SJS/TEN, Aplastic Anemia.

### 2.2 Gabapentinoids (Gabapentin & Pregabalin)
*   **Mechanism:** Bind to the **$\alpha 2\delta$ subunit** of voltage-gated Calcium channels (presynaptic).
    *   Reduces calcium influx $\rightarrow$ reduces glutamate release.
    *   *Note:* Do **NOT** bind GABA receptors despite the name.
*   **Therapeutic Uses:**
    *   **Neuropathic Pain:** Diabetic neuropathy, Post-herpetic neuralgia, Fibromyalgia.
    *   **Seizure:** **Focal Seizures** (Adjunct). *Exacerbates Generalized seizures.*
*   **Adverse Effects:** Sedation, **Peripheral Edema**, Weight gain.

## 3. GABA Modulators (Barbiturates & Benzos)
**Story Mode:**
These drugs work directly on the GABA-A receptor, the brain's primary "off switch." By opening the chloride channel, they hyperpolarize neurons. The distinction is in *how* they open it: Benzos increase **Frequency** (Benzos = Frenz), while Barbiturates increase **Duration** (Barbi-durates).

### 3.1 Phenobarbital
*   **Mechanism:** Barbiturate. Binds allosteric site on GABA-A.
    *   Increases **Duration** of $Cl^-$ channel opening.
    *   *Danger:* At high doses, can open the channel *without* GABA (direct agonist) $\rightarrow$ Coma/Death.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Potent Inducer** of CYP450 enzymes (eats up other drugs).
    *   Long half-life (days).
*   **Therapeutic Uses:**
    *   **Neonatal Seizures** (First line).
    *   Status Epilepticus (Refractory).
*   **Adverse Effects:** Profound sedation, Respiratory Depression, Hyperactivity in children (paradoxical).

### 3.2 Benzodiazepines (Diazepam, Lorazepam, Midazolam)
*   **Mechanism:** Binds allosteric site on GABA-A.
    *   Increases **Frequency** of $Cl^-$ channel opening.
    *   *Safety:* Require GABA to work (Ceiling effect).
*   **Therapeutic Uses:**
    *   **Acute:** **Status Epilepticus (First Line)** - "Abortive therapy".
    *   **Chronic:** Rarely used due to **Tolerance**.
*   **Specific Agents:**
    *   **Lorazepam (IV):** Preferred for Status (stays in brain longer).
    *   **Diazepam (IV/Rectal):** Highly lipid soluble $\rightarrow$ rapid entry $\rightarrow$ rapid **Redistribution** to fat/muscle (short duration of action).
    *   **Midazolam (IM/Buccal/Nasal):** Used when IV access is not available (e.g., EMS).

:::correlate
**"Floppy Baby" Syndrome**
**Benzos in Pregnancy:**
Chronic use of benzodiazepines near delivery can cause neonatal withdrawal and hypotonia.
*   **Presentation:** Neonate with severe hypotonia ("rag doll"), difficulty suckling, apnea, and hypothermia.
*   **Management:** Supportive care; avoid benzos in pregnancy if possible.
:::

## 4. Other Agents

### 4.1 Cannabidiol (Epidiolex)
*   **Mechanism:** Unknown (does not act on CB1/CB2 receptors).
*   **Uses:** Severe childhood epilepsy syndromes:
    *   **Dravet Syndrome**
    *   **Lennox-Gastaut Syndrome**
    *   **Tuberous Sclerosis Complex**
*   **Adverse Effects:** Somnolence, elevated liver enzymes (LFTs).

## 5. Summary Tables

:::highyield
**Drug Selection by Seizure Type**
| Seizure Type | First Line / Effective | Contraindicated / Ineffective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Focal** | Carbamazepine, Phenytoin, Lamotrigine, Levetiracetam | -- |
| **Generalized Tonic-Clonic** | Valproate, Levetiracetam, Lamotrigine, Topiramate | Carbamazepine, Phenytoin (can worsen some generalized types) |
| **Absence** | **Ethosuximide**, Valproate | Phenytoin, Carbamazepine, Gabapentin |
| **Status Epilepticus** | **Benzodiazepines** (Lorazepam) $\rightarrow$ Fosphenytoin/Valproate | -- |
:::

:::highyield
**Key Side Effects & Buzzwords**
| Drug | Buzzword / Key Side Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Valproate** | Neural Tube Defects, Hepatotoxicity, Pancreatitis, Hyperammonemia |
| **Topiramate** | Weight Loss, Kidney Stones, Mental dulling ("Dopeamax") |
| **Levetiracetam** | Agitation/Aggression ("Keppra Rage") |
| **Ethosuximide** | GI Distress (Pain/Nausea) |
| **Phenytoin** | Gingival Hyperplasia, Hirsutism, Zero-order kinetics |
| **Lamotrigine** | **Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (Rash)** |
:::
