## 1. Ischemic Stroke Pathology
**Story Mode:**
Ischemia is a lack of blood flow, which means lack of Oxygen (Hypoxia) and lack of Glucose (Hypoglycemia). The brain is incredibly hungry and has zero reserves.

### 1.1 The Ischemic Cascade (Excitotoxicity)
When neurons run out of energy (ATP), their ion pumps fail.
1.  **Depolarization:** Sodium/Calcium flow IN, Potassium flows OUT.
2.  **Glutamate Dump:** The dying neuron releases a massive amount of **Glutamate** (excitatory neurotransmitter).
3.  **Calcium Influx:** Glutamate opens NMDA channels on neighbors $\rightarrow$ Massive **Calcium** influx.
4.  **Enzyme Activation:** Calcium activates "executioner" enzymes (proteases, lipases) that chew up the cell from the inside.
5.  **Result:** **Liquefactive Necrosis**.

### 1.2 Timeline of Ischemic Changes
| Time | Microscopic Change | Macroscopic Change |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0-12 Hours** | **"Red Neurons"** (Eosinophilic cytoplasm, pyknotic nuclei). First visible sign. | Normal or slight blurring of gray-white junction. |
| **12-24 Hours** | **Neutrophils** infiltrate (Acute inflammation). | Tissue becomes pale and soft. |
| **1-3 Days** | **Macrophages** (Microglia) arrive to clean up the mess. | Swelling/Edema (Risk of Herniation). |
| **1-2 Weeks** | **Reactive Gliosis** (Astrocytes proliferate) + Vascular proliferation. | Liquefactive Necrosis (Mushy cavity). |
| **> 2 Weeks** | **Glial Scar** formation (Astrocytic border). | Cystic cavity (hole in the brain). |

## 2. Stroke Classifications
### 2.1 Thrombotic Stroke
*   **Mechanism:** Clot forms **locally** at the site of infarction.
*   **Cause:** Ruptured Atherosclerotic plaque.
*   **Key Locations:** Carotid bifurcation, MCA origin, Basilar artery.
*   **Presentation:** "Stuttering" onset (symptoms wax and wane).

### 2.2 Embolic Stroke
*   **Mechanism:** Clot travels from **elsewhere**.
*   **Causes:**
    *   **Cardiogenic:** Atrial Fibrillation (Left Atrial Appendage), Post-MI thrombus, Vegetation (Endocarditis).
    *   **Fat Emboli:** Long bone fractures (femur). Triad: **Hypoxemia + Petechial Rash + Confusion**.
*   **Key Features:** Sudden, maximal deficit at onset. Affects multiple vascular territories (Embolic Shower).

### 2.3 Global Ischemia (Watershed Infarcts)
*   **Mechanism:** Systemic Hypotension (Shock, Cardiac Arrest).
*   **Location:** Border zones between ACA/MCA or MCA/PCA.
*   **Histology:** **Laminar Necrosis** (Pyramidal neurons of Hippocampus and Cortex layers 3, 5, & 6 are most vulnerable).

### 2.4 Lacunar Strokes
*   **Mechanism:** **Lipohyalinosis** (Hyaline Arteriolosclerosis) of small penetrating vessels due to **Chronic Hypertension**.
*   **Vessels:** Lenticulostriate arteries.
*   **Location:** Basal Ganglia, Thalamus, Internal Capsule, Pons.
*   **Clinical:** Pure Motor or Pure Sensory deficits. No cortical signs (No aphasia, no neglect).

## 3. Hemorrhagic Strokes & Vascular Malformations

| Condition | Etiology | Key Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Intracerebral Hemorrhage (ICH)** | **Hypertension** (Deep bleeds: Basal Ganglia/Thalamus via **Charcot-Bouchard Microaneurysms**). <br> **Amyloid Angiopathy** (Lobar/Cortical bleeds in elderly). | Rapid onset, focal deficit + Headache + Nausea/Vomiting (High ICP). |
| **Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH)** | **Ruptured Berry Aneurysm** (ACA-Comm is #1 site). Associated with PCKD, Ehlers-Danlos. | **"Thunderclap Headache"**. Nuchal rigidity. Lumbar Puncture: **Xanthochromia** (yellow CSF). |
| **A-V Malformation (AVM)** | Congenital tangle of arteries-to-veins (no capillaries). High flow shunting. | present in **Young Adults** (10-30yo) with **Seizures** or Hemorrhage. |

## 4. Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI)

### 4.1 Skull Fractures
*   **Linear:** Simple crack.
*   **Depressed:** Bone fragment pushed inward (requires surgery).
*   **Diastatic:** Separation of cranial sutures.
*   **Basilar:** Fracture of the skull base.
    *   **Signs:** **Raccoon Eyes** (Periorbital ecchymosis), **Battle Sign** (Mastoid ecchymosis), **CSF Rhinorrhea/Otorrhea** (Halo sign).

### 4.2 Parenchymal Injuries
*   **Concussion:** Reversible altered mental status (amnesia/confusion) with **NO** structural damage on CT/MRI.
*   **Contusion:** Bruising of brain tissue (Hemorrhage + Edema).
    *   **Coup:** Injury at site of impact (Head hits wall).
    *   **Contrecoup:** Injury opposite to impact (Brain sloshes back). Common in frontal/temporal poles.
*   **Diffuse Axonal Injury (DAI):**
    *   **Mechanism:** Shearing/Rotational forces (e.g., High-speed car crash).
    *   **Pathology:** Stretching/tearing of axons in deep white matter (Corpus Callosum/Brainstem).
    *   **Imaging:** Multiple punctate hemorrhages on **MRI (SWI sequence)**. CT often normal.
    *   **Clinical:** Instant coma. Poor prognosis.
*   **Chronic Traumatic Encephalopathy (CTE):** Accumulation of **Tau protein** neurofibrillary tangles after repetitive mild TBI (Boxers, Football). Dementia, aggression, parkinsonism.

## 5. Herniation Syndromes
**The Story:**
The skull is a closed box. If you add volume (Blood, Tumor, Edema), the brain has nowhere to go but... squished against rigid membranes or out the holes.

### 5.1 Subfalcine (Cingulate) Herniation
*   **Movement:** Cingulate gyrus pushed **under** the Falx Cerebri.
*   **Compression:** **Anterior Cerebral Artery (ACA)**.
*   **Clinical:** Contralateral **Leg Weakness**.

### 5.2 Transtentorial (Uncal) Herniation
*   **Movement:** Uncus (Medial Temporal Lobe) pushed **under** the Tentorium Cerebelli.
*   **Compression:**
    1.  **CN III:** Ipsilateral **"Blown Pupil"** (Dilated/Fixed) + "Down and Out" eye. (First sign).
    2.  **Posterior Cerebral Artery (PCA):** Contralateral Homonymous Hemianopsia (Cortical Blindness).
    3.  **Cerebral Peduncle:** Contralateral Hemiparesis (or Ipsilateral if Kernohan's Notch phenomenon).
    4.  **Duret Hemorrhages:** Tearing of paramedian basilar arteries $\rightarrow$ Brainstem bleed $\rightarrow$ Death.

### 5.3 Tonsillar Herniation
*   **Movement:** Cerebellar Tonsils pushed **through** the Foramen Magnum.
*   **Compression:** **Brainstem (Medulla)**.
*   **Clinical:** Cardiorespiratory Arrest (\"Coning\").
