## Introduction & Mechanism of Action (Slide 3)

Local anesthetics (LAs) are diverse agents used to block sensation in a specific area of the body without loss of consciousness. Their primary utility lies in preventing pain transmission during surgical or dental procedures. To understand how they work, we must first appreciate the physiology of the voltage-gated sodium channel. Nerves transmit signals via action potentials, which are propagated by the rapid influx of sodium ions through these channels.

Local anesthetics function by diffusing through the nerve membrane into the axoplasm, where they bind to the intracellular portion of voltage-gated sodium channels. Crucially, they bind preferentially to channels in the **open** and **inactivated** states—a phenomenon known as **state-dependent block**. By stabilizing the inactivated state, they prevent the channel from resetting to the resting state, thereby halting the propagation of action potentials. This blockade is also **use-dependent**, meaning nerves that are firing rapidly (such as pain fibers) are blocked more effectively than resting nerves.

:::highyield
**Mechanism of Action**
- **Target:** Intracellular portion of **Voltage-Gated Na+ Channels**.
- **Binding State:** High affinity for **Open** and **Inactivated** states (State-Dependent).
- **Effect:** Stabilizes the inactivated state $\rightarrow$ prevents Na+ influx $\rightarrow$ blocks action potential propagation.
- **Use-Dependence:** Higher firing frequency = Stronger block (Pain fibers > Motor fibers).
:::

### Susceptibility to Blockade (Slide 22)
Not all nerve fibers are blocked equally. The sensitivity of a nerve fiber to LA blockade is determined by its diameter, myelination, and firing frequency. In general, smaller diameter and higher firing frequency increase sensitivity.

- **Order of Blockade:**
    1.  **Small, myelinated** (Sympathetic fibers) - *Blocked First*
    2.  **Small, unmyelinated** (Pain/Temp - C fibers) & **Small, myelinated** (Pain/Temp - A$\delta$ fibers)
    3.  **Large, myelinated** (Touch, Pressure, Motor) - *Blocked Last*

:::mnemonic
**Fiber Blockade Order**
**"Silly Puns Make Me Laugh"**
- **S**iopathetic (Sympathetic)
- **P**ain (Pain/Temp)
- **T**ouch (Touch/Pressure)
- **M**otor (Motor)
:::

## Structure & Pharmacokinetics (Slide 6)

Local anesthetics consist of a lipophilic aromatic ring connected to a hydrophilic amine by an intermediate chain. This intermediate chain defines the two major classes: **Esters** and **Amides**. This distinction is critical for determining metabolism and allergic potential.

### Esters vs. Amides (Slide 6)
- **Esters:** Metabolized by plasma **pseudocholinesterase** to **PABA** (para-aminobenzoic acid). PABA is a common allergen, making hypersensitivity reactions more common in this class.
- **Amides:** Metabolized in the **liver** by CYP450 enzymes. Hypersensitivity is extremely rare.

:::mnemonic
**Amides have Two I's**
- **Esters (One 'i'):** Procaine, Cocaine, Tetracaine, Benzocaine
- **Amides (Two 'i's):** L**i**doca**i**ne, Mep**i**vaca**i**ne, Bup**i**vaca**i**ne, Rop**i**vaca**i**ne, Pr**i**loca**i**ne
:::

| Feature | Esters | Amides |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Metabolism** | Plasma Pseudocholinesterase | Hepatic CYP450 |
| **Metabolite** | PABA (Allergenic) | Inactive (mostly) |
| **Allergy Risk** | High (due to PABA) | Very Low |
| **Duration** | Short (rapid hydrolysis) | Long (hepatic clearance) |
| **Examples** | Procaine, Tetracaine, Cocaine | Lidocaine, Bupivacaine |

### pH and pKa: The Onset of Action (Slide 18)
Local anesthetics are weak bases (pKa 7.5–9.0). They exist in equilibrium between a non-ionized (lipid-soluble) form and an ionized (water-soluble, charged) form.
- **Non-Ionized Form:** Crosses the nerve membrane (Lipid soluble).
- **Ionized Form:** Binds to the receptor inside the channel (Active form).

**The Infection Concept:**
Infected tissue is **acidic** (low pH). In an acidic environment, the equilibrium shifts towards the **ionized** form ($LA + H^+ \rightarrow LAH^+$). Since the ionized form cannot cross the membrane, the drug cannot reach its target site. Therefore, local anesthetics are **less effective** in infected/acidic tissue.

### Potency, Duration, and Absorption (Slide 19)
- **Potency:** Defined by **Lipid Solubility**. Highly lipophilic drugs (e.g., Bupivacaine) penetrate membranes easily $\rightarrow$ High Potency.
- **Duration:** Defined by **Protein Binding** and **Vascularity**.
    - High Protein Binding = Long Duration (Drug stays at receptor).
    - High Vascularity = Short Duration (Drug washed away).
- **Vasoconstrictors:** **Epinephrine** is often added to LAs to cause vasoconstriction. This decreases systemic absorption, reduces toxicity, and **prolongs the duration of action** by keeping the drug at the site.

:::correlate
**Vasoconstrictor Contraindication**
**Epinephrine** should **NOT** be used in tissues with end-arterial supply (Fingers, Toes, Penis, Nose) due to risk of ischemia and necrosis.
**Obstetrics:** Epinephrine is avoided in labor epidurals as vasoconstriction can compromise uteroplacental blood flow and cause maternal hypertension.
:::

## Clinical Pharmacology of Agents (Slide 9, 10, 37)

### Ester Local Anesthetics
1.  **Procaine:** Prototype, short duration. Metabolized to PABA. Rarely used now.
2.  **Chloroprocaine:** Rapid onset, short duration. Used in epidurals for C-sections.
3.  **Tetracaine:** Long duration, high potency. Used for spinal anesthesia and ophthalmology. *Note:* CSF has little pseudocholinesterase, prolonged action (Slide 36).
4.  **Benzocaine:** Topical only (low pKa, very lipophilic). Used for mucous membranes (e.g., teething, endoscopy).
5.  **Cocaine:** Only LA with intrinsic **vasoconstrictive** properties (blocks NE reuptake). Used Topically for ENT procedures (nose/throat) to shrink mucosa and reduce bleeding.

:::correlate
**Methemoglobinemia (Benzocaine)**
**Benzocaine** (spray/gel) can cause oxidation of iron in hemoglobin ($Fe^{2+} \rightarrow Fe^{3+}$), leading to **Methemoglobinemia**.
- **Presentation:** "Chocolate brown" blood, cyanosis, shortness of breath.
- **Treatment:** Methylene Blue.
:::

### Amide Local Anesthetics
1.  **Lidocaine:** The "Standard". Rapid onset, medium duration. Broad utility (local, spinal, epidural, IV antiarrhythmic).
2.  **Mepivacaine:** Similar to Lidocaine but less vasodilation. Used in dentistry.
3.  **Bupivacaine:** High potency, long duration. Preferred for epidurals post-op pain. **High Cardiotoxicity.**
4.  **Ropivacaine:** Similar to Bupivacaine but **less cardiotoxic** and less motor block (good for labor—"walking epidural").
5.  **Prilocaine:** Medium duration. Risk of methemoglobinemia.

## Toxicity (Systemic & Neural) (Slide 27)

Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity (LAST) occurs when drug levels rise excessively in the blood, usually due to accidental IV injection or excessive dose. The toxicity targets the **CNS** and **Cardiovascular** systems.

### Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity (LAST)
- **Phase 1: CNS Excitation:** Perioral numbness (metallic taste), tinnitus, agitation, seizures.
- **Phase 2: CNS Depression:** Coma, respiratory arrest.
- **Phase 3: CV Collapse:** Hypotension, arrhythmias, cardiac arrest.

:::highyield
**LAST progression:**
Numbness/Tinnitus $\rightarrow$ Seizures $\rightarrow$ Coma $\rightarrow$ Cardiac Arrest.
**Key Concept:** CNS signs usually precede CV signs (Warning sign!), **EXCEPT** with Bupivacaine where they can happen simultaneously.
:::

:::correlate
**Bupivacaine Cardiotoxicity**
**Bupivacaine** has high affinity for cardiac Na+ channels and dissociates slowly ("Fast In, Slow Out").
- **Risk:** Severe refractory arrhythmias and cardiovascular collapse.
- **Avoid:** Do not use for IV regional anesthesia (Bier Block).
- **Rescue:** **Intralipid Emulsion** (Lipid Rescue) acts as a lipid sink to soak up the lipophilic drug.
:::

### Specific Neural Toxicities
1.  **Transient Neurologic Symptoms (TNS):**
    - **Associated with:** **Lidocaine** (Spinal).
    - **Presentation:** Pain/dysesthesia in buttocks/legs resolving in days.
    - **Prognosis:** Full recovery, no permanent damage.
2.  **Cauda Equina Syndrome:**
    - **Associated with:** Lidocaine (Spinal Continuous Microcatheters - historical).
    - **Presentation:** Bowel/bladder dysfunction, saddle anesthesia, paralysis.
    - **Prognosis:** Potential for permanent damage.

### Hematologic Toxicity
- **Methemoglobinemia:** Caused by **Prilocaine** (metabolite o-toluidine) and **Benzocaine**.

### Allergies
- **Esters:** High risk due to **PABA**. Cross-reactivity exists among esters.
- **Amides:** Very rare. No cross-reactivity with esters. If allergic to one amide, may try another (preservative-free).

## Review Questions

**1. A 25-year-old woman in labor requests an epidural. The anesthesiologist selects an agent that provides adequate sensory analgesia but minimizes motor blockade to allow her to participate in labor. Which agent was most likely chosen?**

A. Tetracaine
B. Ropivacaine
C. Procaine
D. Cocaine
E. Benzocaine

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: B (Ropivacaine)**

**Rationale**:
Ropivacaine is a long-acting amide local anesthetic similar to bupivacaine but with **less motor blockade** at analgesic doses (differential block). This makes it ideal for labor epidurals ("walking epidural").
- A, C: Esters are rarely used for epidural infusions.
- D: Cocaine is topical only.
- E: Benzocaine is topical only.

</details>

**2. A patient undergoing a dental procedure receives a potentially toxic dose of a local anesthetic. Which early sign would most likely alert the dentist to impending systemic toxicity?**

A. Cardiovascular collapse
B. Respiratory depression
C. Perioral numbness and metallic taste
D. Profound hypotension
E. Bradycardia

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: C (Perioral numbness and metallic taste)**

**Rationale**:
CNS toxicity typically precedes cardiovascular toxicity (except with bupivacaine where they may be simultaneous). Early symptoms of CNS excitation include **circumoral numbness**, tinnitus, and a metallic taste, followed by nystagmus and seizures.
- A, D, E: These are late cardiovascular signs.
- B: Occurs after the excitation phase.

</details>

**3. Which local anesthetic is an ester that is metabolized to a product known to cause hypersensitivity reactions?**

A. Lidocaine
B. Bupivacaine
C. Mepivacaine
D. Tetracaine
E. Ropivacaine

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: D (Tetracaine)**

**Rationale**:
Tetracaine is an **ester** (one "i"). Esters are metabolized by pseudocholinesterase to **PABA** (para-aminobenzoic acid), which is immunogenic and associated with allergic reactions.
- A, B, C, E: These are Amides (two "i"s) and do not form PABA.

</details>

**4. A child is brought to the ER with cyanosis and shortness of breath after his parents applied a generous amount of a teething gel. Blood appears chocolate-brown. Which agent is implicated?**

A. Lidocaine
B. Benzocaine
C. Bupivacaine
D. Ropivacaine
E. Procaine

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: B (Benzocaine)**

**Rationale**:
**Benzocaine** is a common topical anesthetic implicated in **Methemoglobinemia**, especially in children. It oxidizes hemoglobin Fe2+ to Fe3+, reducing oxygen carrying capacity. Prilocaine is the other major culprit.
- A: Lidocaine can cause CNS/CV toxicity but rarely methemoglobinemia.

</details>

**5. Why is epinephrine often co-administered with lidocaine for infiltration anesthesia?**

A. To increase the pH of the solution
B. To preventing oxidation of lidocaine
C. To reduce the pain of injection
D. To prolong the duration of action via vasoconstriction
E. To block potassium channels

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: D (To prolong the duration of action via vasoconstriction)**

**Rationale**:
Epinephrine is a vasoconstrictor ($\alpha_1$ agonist). It reduces local blood flow, determining the "washout" of the drug. By keeping the drug at the site longer, it **increases duration** and **reduces systemic toxicity**.

</details>
