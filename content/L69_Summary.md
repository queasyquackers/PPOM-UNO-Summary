## Substance Use Disorders (SUD) Overview

Substance Use Discovery involves a complex interaction between brain circuitry, genetics, and environmental factors where an individual continues to use a substance despite significant harm. It is a chronic, relapsing brain disease characterized by compulsive drug seeking and use. The DSM-5 defines SUD based on a pathological pattern of behaviors related to the use of the substance. Diagnosis requires meeting specific criteria within a 12-month period, and severity is graded based on the count of these criteria met: Mild (2-3), Moderate (4-5), and Severe (6+).

### The 11 Criteria (Grouped) (Slide 4)
*   **Impaired Control**
    *   Taking the substance in larger amounts or longer than intended.
    *   Persistent desire or unsuccessful efforts to cut down.
    *   Spending a great deal of time obtaining, using, or recovering from the substance.
    *   **Craving**: A strong desire or urge to use.
*   **Social Impairment**
    *   Recurrent use resulting in failure to fulfill major role obligations (work, school, home).
    *   Continued use despite persistent social or interpersonal problems.
    *   Important social, occupational, or recreational activities are given up.
*   **Risky Use**
    *   Recurrent use in situations where it is physically hazardous (e.g., driving).
    *   Continued use despite knowledge of having a persistent physical or psychological problem caused by the substance.
*   **Pharmacological Criteria**
    *   **Tolerance**: Need for increased amounts to achieve intoxication or diminished effect with the same amount.
    *   **Withdrawal**: The characteristic withdrawal syndrome for the substance or taking the substance to relieve withdrawal.
    *   *Note*: Tolerance and withdrawal do NOT count as criteria if the medication is taken as prescribed under medical supervision (e.g., opioids for chronic pain).

:::highyield
**Medical Use Exception:**
Tolerance and Withdrawal generally do **NOT** count toward the diagnosis of a Substance Use Disorder if the substance is being used solely under appropriate medical supervision (e.g., a patient on chronic opioids for cancer pain).
:::

## Alcohol Use Disorder

Alcohol affects the central nervous system as a depressant. It has profound public health implications, contributing significantly to emergency room visits, overdoses, and deaths annually. Screening and history taking are critical, as patients may minimize their use. The disorder is characterized by the inability to stop or control alcohol use despite adverse social, occupational, or health consequences.

### Alcohol Intoxication (Slide 8-9)
*   **Presentation**:
    *   **Slurred speech**
    *   **Incoordination / Ataxia** (Unsteady gait)
    *   **Nystagmus** (Involuntary eye movement)
    *   Impaired attention or memory
    *   Stupor or Coma
*   **Signs**: Flushed face, **Conjunctival Injection** (also in Cannabis), decreased level of consciousness.

### Alcohol Poisoning (Slide 10-14)
*   **Mechanism**: Alcohol level overwhelms the liver's metabolic capacity.
*   **Signs**:
    *   **Respiratory Depression** (Breathing slows/stops)
    *   Hypothermia, Incontinence, Seizures, Coma.
    *   Loss of gag reflex (risk of aspiration).
*   **Management**:
    *   Airway protection (intubation if necessary).
    *   IV Fluids and Glucose (Check fingerstick first!).
    *   **Thiamine (Vitamin B1)**: Administer **BEFORE** or with glucose to prevent **Wernicke's Encephalopathy**.
    *   **Haloperidol**: Preferred for severe agitation.

### Alcohol Withdrawal (Slide 16-17)
*   **Timeline**:
    *   **Minor Withdrawal (6-36 hours)**: Tremors ("The Shakes"), Anxiety, Headache, Diaphoresis, Palpitations, GI upset.
    *   **Seizures (6-48 hours)**: Generalized tonic-clonic.
    *   **Alcoholic Hallucinosis (12-48 hours)**: Visual/Tactile hallucinations with *intact* orientation.
    *   **Delirium Tremens (DTs) (48-96 hours)**: **MEDICAL EMERGENCY**.
*   **Delirium Tremens (DTs)** (Slide 17):
    *   **Triad**: Delirium (Confusion/Disorientation) + Hallucinations (often visual/tactile "bugs") + **Autonomic Instability** (Fever, Tachycardia, Hypertension).
    *   **Mortality**: High if untreated.

:::correlate
Delirium Tremens (DTs)

- **Pathophysiology:** Chronic alcohol use downregulates GABA receptors and upregulates NMDA (Glutamate) receptors. Abrupt cessation leads to uninhibited excitation (Glutamate storm).
- **Presentation:** Delirium (Confusion), Agitation, Fever, Tachycardia (>100 bpm), Hypertension, Diaphoresis.
- **Diagnosis:** Clinical diagnosis based on history of cessation + hyperadrenergic state.
- **Treatment:** **Benzodiazepines** (Diazepam, Chlordiazepoxide, Lorazepam). IV Fluids.
- **Lecturer's Point:** "Fifteen percent will develop DTs... Treatment is Diazepam."
:::

### Treatment of Alcohol Use Disorder
*   **Detoxification**: Benzodiazepines (Gold Standard).
*   **Maintenance**:
    *   **Naltrexone**: Opioid antagonist; reduces cravings and the "high" from drinking.
    *   **Acamprosate**: NMDA modulator.
    *   **Disulfiram**: Aldehyde dehydrogenase inhibitor (Causes sickness if drinking).

## Opioid Use Disorder (Slide 26-36)

Opioids bind to mu-opioid receptors to produce analgesia and euphoria. The epidemic of opioid misuse involves natural (Morphine, Codeine), semi-synthetic (Heroin, Oxycodone), and synthetic (Fentanyl, Methadone) varieties. Fentanyl is particularly dangerous due to its extreme potency (50-100x morphine) and its clandestine addition to other street drugs.

### Opioid Intoxication
*   **Classic Triad**:
    1.  **Coma** / CNS Depression
    2.  **Respiratory Depression** (Lethal mechanism)
    3.  **Miosis** (Pinpoint Pupils)
*   **Other Signs**: Constipation, Slurred speech, Impairment in attention.

### Opioid Withdrawal
*   **Mechanism**: Loss of inhibitory opioid tone leads to Noradrenergic (NE) storm.
*   **Presentation**: "Flu-like" illness. *Not life-threatening* (unlike Alcohol/Benzo withdrawal), but extremely uncomfortable.
*   **Symptoms**:
    *   **Dysphoria** & Craving.
    *   **Rhinorrhea** (Runny nose) & **Lacrimation** (Tearing).
    *   **Yawning**.
    *   **Piloerection** ("Gooseflesh" -> term "Cold Turkey").
    *   **Mydriasis** (Dilated pupils).
    *   Muscle aches, Diarrhea, Nausea/Vomiting.

:::mnemonic
**Opioid Withdrawal: SLUDGE stops, flight begins**
**Symptoms are the opposite of intoxication**
- **Intoxication:** Constipation, Miosis, Dry.
- **Withdrawal:** Diarrhea, Mydriasis, Rhinorrhea, Lacrimation, Yawning.
:::

### Treatment of Opioid Use Disorder (Slide 37)
*   **Overdose**: **Naloxone** (Mu-antagonist). Intranasal formulation is widely available.
*   **Maintenance Therapy (MAT)**:
    1.  **Methadone**: **Full Mu-Agonist**. Long half-life. Reduces cravings and withdrawal. Requires daily clinic visits.
    2.  **Buprenorphine**: **Partial Mu-Agonist**. High affinity, low intrinsic activity. Ceiling effect creates safety profile. Can be prescribed in office (Suboxone = Buprenorphine + Naloxone).
    3.  **Naltrexone**: **Mu-Antagonist**. Blocks opioid effects. Patient must be fully detoxed first to avoid precipitated withdrawal.

## Stimulant Use Disorder (Cocaine, Amphetamines) (Slide 44-53)

Stimulants increase synaptic levels of Dopamine, Norepinephrine, and Serotonin. Cocaine blocks reuptake; Amphetamines increase release. The clinical picture is one of sympathetic overdrive.

### Stimulant Intoxication
*   **Psychological**: Euphoria, **Agitation**, Hypervigilance, Grandiosity, Paranoia.
*   **Physical**:
    *   **Mydriasis** (Dilated pupils).
    *   **Tachycardia** / Hypertension.
    *   **Chest Pain** (Coronary vasospasm).
    *   Weight loss, Chills, Sweating.
*   **Complications**: Cardiac Arrest, Seizures, Stroke.

### Stimulant Withdrawal ("The Crash")
*   **Symptoms**:
    *   **Dysphoria** / Severe Depression (Suicide risk).
    *   **Fatigue** / Psychomotor retardation.
    *   **Hypersomnia** with **Vivid, unpleasant dreams**.
    *   **Increased Appetite**.

## Cannabis Use Disorder (Slide 22-24)

Cannabis acts on CB1 and CB2 receptors. It is the most commonly used illicit drug. Modern strains have very high THC content, leading to higher risks of acute psychosis and anxiety.

### Cannabis Intoxication
*   **Psychological**: Euphoria ("High"), Anxiety, Sensation of slowed time, Impaired judgment and motor coordination.
*   **Physical Signs**:
    *   **Conjunctival Injection** (**Red Eyes** - Hallmark).
    *   **Increased Appetite** ("The Munchies").
    *   **Dry Mouth** ("Cotton mouth").
    *   Tachycardia.

### Cannabis Withdrawal
*   **Timeline**: Starts within ~1 week of cessation.
*   **Symptoms**:
    *   **Irritability**, Anger, Aggression.
    *   **Anxiety** / Restlessness.
    *   **Insomnia** / Disturbing dreams.
    *   Decreased appetite / Weight loss.
    *   Depressed mood.

## Nicotine Use Disorder (Slide 19-21)

Nicotine stimulates nicotinic acetylcholine receptors, releasing Dopamine (reward). It is highly addictive, with relapse rates similar to heroin.

### Nicotine Withdrawal
*   **Symptoms**:
    *   **Irritability**, Frustration, Anger.
    *   **Anxiety**.
    *   **Difficulty Concentrating**.
    *   **Increased Appetite** / Weight Gain.
    *   Restlessness, Insomnia, Depressed mood.

### Treatment (Smoking Cessation)
*   **Varenicline (Chantix)**: (Slide 20)
    *   **Mechanism**: **Partial Agonist** at alpha-4-beta-2 nicotinic receptors.
    *   **Effect**: Reduces cravings (agonist activity) AND blocks reward from smoking (antagonist activity).
    *   **Efficacy**: **Most effective** monotherapy.
*   **Bupropion (Zyban)**: (Slide 20)
    *   **Mechanism**: NE/DA Reuptake Inhibitor (NDRI).
    *   **Use**: Reduces cravings. Contraindicated in seizure disorders.
*   **Nicotine Replacement Therapy (NRT)**: Patch, Gum, Lozenge (Agonists).

## Sedative-Hypnotic Use Disorder (Benzodiazepines, Barbiturates) (Slide 38-42)

Mechanism involves potentiation of GABA-A receptors (inhibitory). The clinical profile of intoxication and withdrawal mirrors alcohol almost exactly.

### Intoxication
*   Slurred speech, Incoordination, Unsteady gait, Nystagmus, Stupor/Coma.
*   **Danger**: Respiratory depression, especially when combined with alcohol (Synergistic effect).

### Withdrawal
*   **Warning**: **Life-Threatening** (Like alcohol).
*   **Symptoms**: Autonomic hyperactivity (Sweating, Pulse >100), Hand tremor, Insomnia, Nausea.
*   **Severe**: **Grand Mal Seizures**, Delirium.

## Hallucinogen Use Disorder (PCP, LSD) (Slide 24)

### Phencyclidine (PCP) Intoxication
*   **Behavior**: **Belligerence**, excessive impulsivity, **Violence**, unpredictability.
*   **Physical Signs**:
    *   **Nystagmus** (Vertical or Horizontal) - *Key Differentiator*.
    *   **Analgesia** (Diminished response to pain) -> "Superhuman strength".
    *   Hypertension, Tachycardia, Seizures.

### LSD (Lysergic Acid Diethylamide)
*   **Signs**: Perceptual changes (depersonalization, derealization, illusions), Mydriasis, Tachycardia. Not usually violent like PCP.

## Substance Intoxication & Withdrawal Comparison Table

| Substance | Intoxication Signs | Withdrawal Signs | Key Life Risks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Alcohol** | Slurred speech, Ataxia, Nystagmus | Tremor, **DTs** (Autonomic instability), Seizures | **Withdrawal is Fatal** |
| **Opioids** | **Miosis**, Resp Depression, Coma | **Mydriasis**, Rhinorrhea, Flu-like, Yawning | Overdose is Fatal |
| **Stimulants** | **Mydriasis**, Tachycardia, Chest Pain | Depression ("Crash"), Hunger, Sleep | Cardiac Arrest (Intox) |
| **Cannabis** | **Red Eyes**, Munchies, Dry Mouth | Irritability, Insomnia, No Appetite | Impaired Driving |
| **Benzos** | Same as Alcohol | Same as Alcohol (Seizures) | **Withdrawal is Fatal** |
| **PCP** | **Nystagmus** (Vert/Horiz), **Violence** | N/A | Trauma/Violence |
