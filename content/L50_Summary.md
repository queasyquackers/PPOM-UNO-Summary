# Lecture #50: Seizures and Epilepsy



## 1. Definitions & Pathophysiology
**Story Mode:**
Classically, we think of a seizure as a "storm" in the brain. Physiologically, this is an **imbalance between Excitation (Glutamate) and Inhibition (GABA)**. Too much gas or not enough brakes leads to hypersynchronous, aberrant neuronal firing.
*   **Seizure:** Transient occurrence of signs/symptoms due to abnormal excessive or synchronous neuronal activity.
*   **Epilepsy:** A disease of the brain defined by:
    1.  At least **two unprovoked** seizures >24 hours apart.
    2.  One unprovoked seizure with a **high probability** (>60%) of recurrence (e.g., structural lesion on MRI).
    3.  Diagnosis of an Epilepsy Syndrome.

### 1.1 The "VITAMINS" of Etiology
Common causes of seizures to remember:
*   **V**ascular (Stroke, especially in elderly)
*   **I**nfection (Meningitis, Encephalitis)
*   **T**rauma (TBI)
*   **A**utoimmune (Anti-NMDA)
*   **M**etabolic (Hypoglycemia, Hyponatremia)
*   **I**ngestion/Withdrawal (Alcohol withdrawal, Benzos)
*   **N**eoplasm (Tumors)
*   **S**ychogenic (Pseudo-seizures / PNES)

## 2. Seizure Semiology & Classification
**Story Mode:**
The old terms "Grand Mal" and "Petit Mal" are out. We now classify based on **Onset** (Focal vs. Generalized) and **Awareness**.

### 2.1 Focal Seizures (Start in one hemisphere)
*   **Focal Aware (Old: Simple Partial):**
    *   Neuron storm is limited to one spot. Patient is **Awake/Aware**.
    *   **Jacksonian March:** Activity "marches" along the motor strip (Thumb $\rightarrow$ Hand $\rightarrow$ Arm).
    *   **Aura:** A focal aware seizure manifesting as a subjective sensation (e.g., chemical smell, rising epigastric sensation, fear) before spreading.
*   **Focal Impaired Awareness (Old: Complex Partial):**
    *   Seizure affects consciousness/mentation.
    *   **Automatisms:** Repetitive, non-purposeful movements (Lip smacking, picking at clothes).
    *   **Localization:** Most common in **Temporal Lobe** (Hippocampus/Amygdala).

### 2.2 Generalized Seizures (Both hemispheres involved)
*   **Generalized Tonic-Clonic (GTC):**
    *   **Tonic Phase:** Stiffening, "Epileptic Cry" (air forced through closed cords), Cyanosis.
    *   **Clonic Phase:** Rhythmic jerking.
    *   **Postictal:** Limp, unresponsive, loud snoring (airway risk!).
*   **Absence Seizures (Old: Petit Mal):**
    *   **Clinically:** Sudden behavioral arrest, "blank stare", <15 seconds. No postictal confusion.
    *   **Triggers:** **Hyperventilation**.
    *   **Mechanism:** Hyperpolarization of Thalamocortical Relay neurons leads to de-inactivation of **T-Type Calcium Channels** $\rightarrow$ Burst firing.
    *   **Gene:** **CACNA1H**.
    *   **EEG:** **3 Hz Spike and Wave** (Pathognomonic).
    *   **Treatment:** **Ethosuximide**.

:::correlate
**Status Epilepticus**
**Definition:**
*   Seizure lasting **> 5 minutes** (convulsive) or **> 10 minutes** (non-convulsive).
*   OR Two seizures without return to baseline in between.
**Management:**
*   **Medical Emergency!** "Time is Brain" (Excitotoxicity).
*   **1st Line:** Benzodiazepines (IV **Lorazepam**/Ativan).
:::

## 3. Epilepsy Syndromes (Genetics & Structural)
**Story Mode:**
We are moving away from "Idiopathic" epilepsy effectively. Advanced genetics now link specific channelopathies to specific syndromes.

### 3.1 Ion Channelopathies
*   **Dravet Syndrome:**
    *   **Gene:** **SCN1A** (Nav1.1 Sodium Channel loss of function in inhibitory interneurons).
    *   **Presentation:** Febrile seizures in infancy $\rightarrow$ Refractory epilepsy, developmental regression. High mortality (SUDEP).
*   **EAST Syndrome:**
    *   **Acronym:** **E**pilepsy, **A**taxia, **S**ensorineural deafness, **T**ubulopathy (Kidney).
    *   **Gene:** **KCNJ10** (Kir4.1 Potassium channel in **Astrocytes**).
    *   **Mechanism:** Astrocytes can't buffer extracellular K+ $\rightarrow$ Neurons depolarize.

### 3.2 Structural & Neurocutaneous Syndromes
*   **Tuberous Sclerosis Complex (TSC):**
    *   **Genes:** **TSC1** (Hamartin) or **TSC2** (Tuberin).
    *   **Pathway:** Loss of inhibition of **mTOR** pathway $\rightarrow$ Uncontrolled cell growth (Hamartomas).
    *   **Clinical:**
        *   **Brain:** Cortical Tubers (cause epilepsy), Subependymal Nodules.
        *   **Skin:** **Ash Leaf Spots** (Hypopigmented), Shagreen Patches, Facial Angiofibromas.
        *   **Heart:** Rhabdomyoma.
        *   **Kidney:** Angiomyolipoma.
*   **Sturge-Weber Syndrome:**
    *   **Gene:** **GNAQ** (Somatic Mosaicism, NOT hereditary).
    *   **Clinical:** **Port-Wine Stain** (V1/V2 distribution), Leptomeningeal Angioma, Glaucoma, Seizures.
*   **Lennox-Gastaut Syndrome (LGS):**
    *   **Presentation:** Childhood (<8yo). Intellectual disability.
    *   **Seizures:** Multiple types (**Atonic/Drop Attacks**, Tonic).
    *   **EEG:** **Slow Spike & Wave (< 2.5 Hz)**.
    *   **Gene:** **GABRB3** (GABA receptor subunit).

### 3.3 Autoimmune & Encephalitis
*   **Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis:**
    *   **Antibody:** IgG against **NR1 subunit** of NMDA receptor.
    *   **Demographics:** Young women. Associated with **Ovarian Teratoma**.
    *   **Course:** Viral prodrome $\rightarrow$ Psychosis/Hallucinations $\rightarrow$ Catatonia/Mute $\rightarrow$ Hyperkinetic (Orofacial Dyskinesia).
*   **Rasmussen Encephalitis:**
    *   **Pathology:** T-Cell mediated chronic encephalitis affecting **one hemisphere**.
    *   **Outcome:** Progressive atrophy and hemiparesis.
    *   **Treatment:** **Hemispherectomy** (Functional hemispherectomy, usually in children due to neuroplasticity).

## 4. Workup & Treatment Modalities
### 4.1 Diagnostics
*   **EEG:** Gold standard. Can use Hyperventilation or Photic Stimulation to provoke.
*   **Imaged:**
    *   **CT:** Emergency (Rule out bleed/mass). Quick.
    *   **MRI:** Elective/Detailed. Looks for Mesial Temporal Sclerosis, Cortical Dysplasia (Lissencephaly, Heterotopia).

### 4.2 Treatments
*   **Pharmacology:** Goal is seizure freedom with minimal side effects.
*   **Ketogenic Diet:** High Fat, Low Carb. Mechanism specific unclear but effective in peds.
*   **Surgical:**
    *   **Resection:** Lesionectomy or Lobectomy (e.g., for Temporal Lobe Epilepsy).
    *   **Corpus Callosotomy:** Palliative. Prevents spread (generalization) of seizures protecting against drop attacks. Spares the Splenium to preserve some function.
*   **Neuromodulation:**
    *   **Vagal Nerve Stimulator (VNS):** Pacemaker for the vagus nerve.
    *   **RNS (Responsive Neurostimulation):** Closed-loop system. Detects seizure pattern $\rightarrow$ Zaps it to abort.
    *   **DBS (Deep Brain Stimulation):** Anterior Nucleus of Thalamus.

:::highyield
**The "Do Not Miss" Associations**
| Syndrome | Gene/Antibody | Key Feature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dravet** | **SCN1A** | Febrile Szs $\rightarrow$ Regression |
| **Absence** | **CACNA1H** | 3Hz Spike/Wave, Ethosuximide |
| **Lennox-Gastaut** | **GABRB3** | Atonic (Drop) Szs, Slow Spike/Wave |
| **Tuberous Sclerosis** | **TSC1/TSC2** | Cortical Tubers, Ash Leaf Spots, mTOR |
| **Anti-NMDA** | **Anti-NR1** | Ovarian Teratoma, Psychiatric onset |
:::

## 7. Review Questions
**1. A 6-year-old boy is brought to the pediatrician by his teacher, who reports he "zones out" multiple times a day for 10 seconds. He does not respond to his name during these episodes but immediately resumes activity afterward. Hyperventilation in the office reproduces the event. Which of the following is the most likely mechanism of this disorder?**

A. Loss of GABA-A receptor function
B. SCN1A sodium channel mutation
C. T-type calcium channel hypersynchrony
D. Glutamate excitotoxicity via NMDA receptors
E. Potassium channel accumulation in astrocytes

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: C (T-type calcium channel hypersynchrony)**

**Rationale**:
This is **Absence Epilepsy**. The hallmark is a brief (<15s) stare triggered by hyperventilation with no postictal state. The pathophysiology involves hyperpolarization of thalamocortical relay neurons leading to rebound burst firing via **T-type Calcium Channels**. Treatment is Ethosuximide.

</details>

**2. A 24-year-old female presents with new-onset seizures and acute psychosis. She has been acting strangely, with fluctuating catatonia and agitation. Physical exam reveals rhythmic tongue-smacking movements. An MRI of the pelvis is ordered. What is the physician looking for?**

A. Renal Angiomyolipoma
B. Ovarian Teratoma
C. Uterine Fibroids
D. Polycystic Ovaries
E. Bladder transitional cell carcinoma

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: B (Ovarian Teratoma)**

**Rationale**:
This clinical picture (Psychosis + Seizures + Orofacial Dyskinesia in a young woman) is classic for **Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis**. It is a paraneoplastic syndrome strongly associated with **Ovarian Teratomas**.

</details>

**3. A 4-month-old infant presents with a prolonged febrile seizure. Over the next year, he develops multiple seizure types including myoclonic and atonic seizures, and shows signs of developmental regression. Genetic testing reveals a mutation in SCN1A. What is the most likely diagnosis?**

A. West Syndrome
B. Lennox-Gastaut Syndrome
C. Dravet Syndrome
D. Juvenile Myoclonic Epilepsy
E. Benign Rolandic Epilepsy

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: C (Dravet Syndrome)**

**Rationale**:
**Dravet Syndrome** is a severe myoclonic epilepsy of infancy caused by an **SCN1A** mutation. It classically begins with prolonged febrile seizures in the first year of life and progresses to intractable epilepsy and intellectual disability.

</details>

**4. A patient with intractable epilepsy undergoes a corpus callosotomy. Which of the following structures is typically spared during this procedure to preserve inter-hemispheric transfer of visual information?**

A. Rostrum
B. Genu
C. Body
D. Splenium
E. Anterior Commissure

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: D (Splenium)**

**Rationale**:
The **Splenium** (posterior portion) of the corpus callosum is often spared (anterior 2/3rds callosotomy) to preserve the transfer of visual information between the occipital lobes, maintaining some functional connectivity while preventing the generalization of seizures.

</details>

**5. Which of the following EEG findings is paired with the correct Epilepsy Syndrome?**

A. 3 Hz Spike and Wave - Lennox Gastaut Syndrome
B. Hypsarrhythmia - Childhood Absence Epilepsy
C. Slow (<2.5 Hz) Spike and Wave - Lennox Gastaut Syndrome
D. Centrotemporal Spikes - Dravet Syndrome
E. Generalized Polyspike - Focal Temporal Lobe Epilepsy

<details>
<summary><strong>Click to Reveal Answer</strong></summary>

**Answer: C (Slow (<2.5 Hz) Spike and Wave - Lennox Gastaut Syndrome)**

**Rationale**:
**Lennox-Gastaut Syndrome** is characterized by a **Slow Spike and Wave** pattern (<2.5 Hz) on EEG. (Note: 3 Hz Spike/Wave is Absence. Hypsarrhythmia is West Syndrome. Centrotemporal spikes are Rolandic Epilepsy).

</details>
