# Lecture 70: Pediatric H&P I: Birth to 5 Years

## Story Mode: The First Checkup
Imagine you are a new pediatric resident. You walk into the room to see a 2-week-old neonate, "Baby Liam," brought in by his exhausted first-time parents. They have a list of questions: "Is his breathing too fast?" "What is this rash on his face?" "Why are his eyes crossed sometimes?" "Is he eating enough?"

This lecture is your guide to navigating these visits, from the very first newborn exam to the preschool years. You aren't just checking boxes; you are monitoring the incredible trajectory of human development, catching silent killers (like biliary atresia or congenital heart defects), and reassuring parents that yes, that "rash" (erythema toxicum) is completely normal.

## The Newborn Exam: Head to Toe
The newborn exam is likely the most thorough physical exam you will perform. Why? Because babies can't tell you what's wrong. You must be the detective.

### 1. Vitals & Measurements ("The Fifth Vital Sign")
*   **Heart Rate:** fast! Normal is **120-160 bpm**.
*   **Respiratory Rate:** Fast! Normal is **30-60 breaths/min**.
*   **Growth Charts:** These are crucial.
    *   **WHO Growth Charts:** Use for **0-2 years**.
    *   **CDC Growth Charts:** Use for **2+ years**.
    *   **Fenton Charts:** Specifically for **preterm infants**.
    *   We track **Length, Weight, and Head Circumference**. Dropping percentiles is a red flag.

### 2. HEENT
*   **Head:**
    *   **Fontanelles:** The "soft spots". **Anterior** closes by **12-15 months**; **Posterior** closes by **1 month**.
        *   *Clinical Correlate:* Sunken = Dehydration. Bulging = Increased ICP (meningitis, hydrocephalus).
    *   **Shape:**
        *   **Caput Succedaneum:** Edema that **crosses suture lines** (benign, resolves in days).
        *   **Cephalohematoma:** Blood under periosteum that does **NOT** cross suture lines (resolves in weeks, risk of jaundice).
        *   **Subgaleal Hemorrhage:** Crosses suture lines, massive blood loss possible (medical emergency).
        *   **Craniosynostosis:** Premature fusion of sutures requiring surgery (e.g., Sagittal = Scaphocephaly/Boat head).
        *   **Plagiocephaly:** Flat spot from positioning (tummy time helps).
*   **Eyes:**
    *   **Red Reflex:** Must be present bilaterally.
        *   *Red Flag:* **Leukocoria** (White reflex) = Retinoblastoma or Congenital Cataracts.
    *   **Alignment:** Intermittent strabismus is normal up to **2-4 months**.
    *   **Conjunctivitis:**
        *   **Chemical:** <24 hrs (silver nitrate).
        *   **Gonococcal:** 2-5 days. Purulent, severe. Prevent w/ Erythromycin ointment. Treat w/ IM Ceftriaxone.
        *   **Chlamydia:** 5-14 days. Watery/mucoid. Treat w/ Oral Erythromycin/Azithromycin.
        *   **HSV:** Keratitis (dendrites).
*   **Ears:** Check for pits/tags (renal association if other anomalies present) and position (Low set = Down Syndrome).
*   **Nose:** Obligate nose breathers. **Choanal Atresia** (bony blockage, cyanosis with feeding) prevents breathing.
*   **Mouth:** Palpate hard/soft palate for clefts. **Epstein Pearls** (benign white cysts) are normal.

### 3. Chest & Lungs
*   **Observation:** Look for retractions or nasal flaring (distress). **Pectus Excavatum** (indented chest) or **Carinatum** (pigeon chest).
*   **Breasts:** **Witch's Milk** (discharge) or hypertrophy is normal due to maternal hormones.
*   **Clavicles:** Palpate for crepitus/step-offs. Fractures are common from birth trauma; treat by pinning sleeve to chest (immobilization).

### 4. Cardiovascular
*   **Murmurs:** Very common.
    *   **Innocent:** Systolic, soft (<3/6), position-dependent (louder supine), asymptomatic.
    *   **Pathologic:** Diastolic, loud, holosystolic, clicks, fixed split S2, symptomatic (cyanosis, failure to thrive).
*   **Pulses:** Palpate **Femoral** and **Brachial**.
    *   *Clinical Correlate:* Weak/absent femoral pulses = **Coarctation of the Aorta**.

### 5. Abdomen & Cord
*   **Umbilical Cord:** Should have **2 Arteries, 1 Vein** (AVA). Single umbilical artery implies renal/genetic anomalies.
    *   Falls off in 10-14 days. Delayed separation (>2 weeks) = **Leukocyte Adhesion Deficiency**.
    *   **Omphalitis:** Erythema around stump = IV antibiotics widely.
    *   **Granuloma:** Pink tissue after cord falls off = Treat w/ Silver Nitrate.
    *   **Hernia:** Umbilical hernias usually resolve by age 3-5.
*   **Masses:** Wilms Tumor (does not cross midline), Neuroblastoma (crosses midline).

### 6. Genitourinary (GU)
*   **Male:** Check for **Cryptorchidism** (undescended testes) and urethral opening.
    *   **Hypospadias:** Ventral (bottom). **Do NOT circumcise** (foreskin used for repair).
    *   **Epispadias:** Dorsal (top) - associated with bladder exstrophy.
    *   **Hydrocele:** Fluid in ******* (transilluminates).
*   **Female:** Vaginal discharge/bleeding is normal (maternal estrogen withdrawal). Fused ***** may need cream.
*   **Ambiguous Genitalia:** Medical emergency! Rule out **Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH)** (salt-wasting crisis).

### 7. Musculoskeletal (The Hips)
*   Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip (DDH). Risk factors: **Female, Breech, Family History**.
*   **Barlow:** Adduct & Push posterior --> Dislocates ("Clunk").
*   **Ortolani:** Abduct & Pull anterior --> Relocates ("Clunk").
*   **Galeazzi:** Knee height discrepancy.
*   *Imaging:* Ultrasound < 4 months; X-ray > 4 months.

### 8. Skin (The Scary but Benign)
*   **Erythema Toxicum Neonatorum:** "Flea-bitten" appearance (pustules on red base). Eosinophils. Benign.
*   **Transient Pustular Melanosis:** Pustules that rupture leaving hyperpigmented macules. Benign.
*   **Milia:** Tiny white sebaceous cysts on nose. Benign.
*   **Nevus Simplex:** "Stork bite" (nape) or "Angel kiss" (glabella). Fades.
*   **Mongolian Spots (Congenital Dermal Melanocytosis):** Blue-grey macules on back/buttocks. Benign (document to differentiate from abuse).
*   **Hemangioma:** Vascular tumor. Grows then involutes. Treat with **Propranolol** if obstructing vision/airway.
*   **Seborrheic Dermatitis:** "Cradle Cap". Greasy scales. Treat with oil/brushing.

### 9. Neuro (Primitive Reflexes)
These should appear at birth and disappear to allow voluntary movement. Persistence often indicates CP or neuro deficit.

| Reflex | Description | Disappears By |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Moro (Startle)** | Drop head back -> Arms extend/abduct then adduct | 4 months |
| **Rooting** | Stroke cheek -> Turns head to side | 4 months |
| **Palmar Grasp** | Finger in palm -> Hand grasps | 3 months |
| **Plantar Grasp** | Pressure on sole -> Toes curl | 8-15 months |
| **ATNR (Fencing)** | Turn head -> Face arm extends, occiput arm flexes | 6 months |
| **Galant** | Stroke spine -> Hips swing to side | 4-6 months |

## Health Maintenance & Guidance

### Nutrition
*   **Breastfeeding:** Gold standard. Contraindications: **Galactosemia, HIV (in US), Active TB, Chemo/Radiation, Ebola, Street drugs**.
    *   Breastfed babies need **Vitamin D** supplements.
*   **Solids:** Introduce **4-6 months**.
    *   *Note:* Early introduction of peanuts (4-6mo) *decreases* allergy risk.
*   **Cow's Milk:** Start at **1 year**. Max **24 oz/day** (prevents Iron Deficiency Anemia).

### Screening Schedule
*   **Newborn:** Metabolic screen (PKU, Hypothyroidism, Sickle Cell, etc), Hearing, CCHD (Critical Congenital Heart Disease).
*   **Anemia & Lead:** **12 months** (and 24mo if high risk).
*   **Autism (M-CHAT):** **18 months** and **24 months**.
*   **Vision & Hearing:** Objective testing starts at **3-4 years**.
*   **Blood Pressure:** Starts at **3 years**.

### Vaccinations (Snapshot)
*   **Birth:** **Hep B**.
*   **2, 4, 6 Months:** **Dr. Hip** (**D**TaP, **R**otavirus, **H**ib, **I**PV, **P**CV).
*   **12-15 Months:** **M**ad **H**elicopter **V** ( **M**MR, **H**ep A, **V**aricella).
*   **4-6 Years:** Boosters (DTaP, IPV, MMR, Varicella).
*   **11-12 Years:** **M**en **T**weens **H**ave **H**PV (**M**eningococcal, **T**dap, **H**PV).

## Clinical Pearls
> [!TIP]
> **Red vs. White vs. Black Stool**
> *   **Black (Meconium):** Normal for first few days.
> *   **White (Acholic):** **Biliary Atresia** (Emergency!).
> *   **Red (Currant Jelly):** Intussusception or Milk Protein Allergy.

> [!WARNING]
> **The Bilious Vomiter**
> Green (bilious) vomiting in a neonate is **Volvulus** (malrotation) until proven otherwise. This is a surgical emergency.

> [!NOTE]
> **Car Seat Safety**
> *   **Rear-facing:** Until age 2 (or max ht/wt).
> *   **Booster:** Until 4'9" (usually 8-12 years).
