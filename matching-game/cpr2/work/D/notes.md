# D notes - lecture conflicts and things added beyond the lecture

## Conflicts / simplifications
- Epiglottitis: CV109 ranks the causes group A strep > S. aureus > Hib (Hib in the unimmunized). The bug item pairs Hib with an UNIMMUNIZED toddler (the lecture case), so it agrees with both the lecture and the classic board pairing.
- Osler nodes: CV105/CV108 call them immune-complex lesions; CV91 says the newer view favors emboli. Cards only describe "tender red nodules on finger pads" and never state a mechanism, so no conflict is repeated.
- Roth spots: CV105/CV108 call them embolic, Duke image calls them immunologic. Not used on any card.
- Prosthetic valve IE timing comes from CV91 (early 1-2 months = skin staph; late >= 1 year = streptococci). CV105 only says S. epidermidis = prosthetic valve IE. Bug items split early (S. epidermidis) vs late (viridans), consistent with both.
- CV108 slide calls emboli from sterile marantic vegetations "septic" - the NBTE card avoids that word.
- Meconium aspiration: slide says "primary pulmonary hypertension"; cards describe the shunt physiology (pre/post-ductal SpO2 gap) instead of using either label.
- Pleurodynia (Coxsackie B) is in CV107 as a lecturer-emphasis aside only.

## Standard facts added where the lecture is silent (none contradict it)
- Croup organism: lectures name croup and list parainfluenza only as a bronchiolitis/asthma trigger; parainfluenza as the croup cause is standard.
- Lobar pneumonia organism: CV104 does not name the bug; S. pneumoniae is standard and CV109 lists it.
- Legionella, B. henselae (cat scratch) and B. quintana (trench fever) are named on CV105 slide 10 as "own diseases" only; vignette details (hotel water, hyponatremia, kitten scratch, louse-borne 5-day fevers) are standard.
- Candida/lemon-juice heroin and Pseudomonas/tap-water injection are standard risk associations used to separate the three PWID organisms the lecture ranks (S. aureus > Candida > Pseudomonas).
- Coxiella phase I IgG >= 1:800 (modified Duke major criterion) - lecture just says serology/PCR.
- Myxoma constitutional symptoms (fever, weight loss, high ESR from IL-6) and positional symptoms: lecture lists obstruction, emboli, conduction anomalies only.
- Pericarditis management (NSAID + colchicine): lecture says "symptom management and supportive care" for viral pericarditis.

## Session 2 (parts 15-19, CV110/CV112 correlates)
- Chronic bronchitis: plan.md row said "3 consecutive years"; CV112 slide 21/57 says cough + sputum >= 3 months in >= 2 consecutive years. Card says "at least 3 months every year for 5 years", which satisfies the lecture definition.
- AERD: lecture calls it "aspirin-sensitive / drug-induced asthma". Leukotriene E4, COX-1 challenge, acetaminophen/celecoxib tolerance and desensitization are standard additions.
- Noonan: lecture says "coagulopathy"; the card's factor XI deficiency/long PTT is the standard specific form. Pulmonary valve stenosis first, HCM second, as the lecturer ranks them.
- CHARGE: lecture lists the acronym only; cyclic cyanosis relieved by crying, CT of the choanae and the oral airway are standard.
- Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency is also taught in CV110 (genetics, augmentation if FEV1 < 65%, transplant) - added CV110 to its lectures.
- Pneumothorax management (needle decompression if unstable, VATS bullectomy/pleurodesis after recurrence) is standard; lecture shows only the chest tube.
- Status asthmaticus: lecture says intubate when pH is normal or acidotic - card's ABG shows the alkalosis-to-acidosis swing.
- Centriacinar emphysema and chronic bronchitis added (CV112 teaches both at length). Both carry avoid entries for E's "COPD exacerbation"; chronic bronchitis also avoids B's "Chronic respiratory acidosis from CO2 retention" and E's "Cor pulmonale", plus centriacinar emphysema on management (same COPD regimen).
- Primary ciliary dyskinesia (Kartagener) added from CV112 slide 46 (lecturer emphasis on AR inheritance, situs inversus, male infertility) - separates CF from post-infectious bronchiectasis.
- Bug disease labels trimmed to disease names (21 changed): micro facts (vitamin B6, slow-growing, skin rods, mucoid) and buzz exposures (lemon juice, water-borne, livestock, lice, membranous parenthetical signs) removed.
