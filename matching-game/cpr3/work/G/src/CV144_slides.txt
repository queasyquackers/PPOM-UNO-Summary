--- SLIDE 43 ---
Source: UpToDate, Amboss
• Union for international cancer control (UICC) determined Lung 
Ca stage from the  Tumor, Node, Metastasis (TNM) system:
• (T) size and extent of the main Tumor 
• (N) Number of nearby lymph nodes involved 
• (M) presence of distal Metastasis
As soon as distant metastases (M1) are detected, the cancer is classified 
as UICC stage IV

How do we stage lung 
cancer?

--- SLIDE 46 ---
• CT scan (chest, liver, adrenal glands) to evaluate
• Nodules: localization, size, margins, calcifications
• Regional spread (hilar lymph nodes, mediastinal invasion, pleural effusions)
• Distant metastasis (hepatic lesions, adrenal gland mass)
• Further radiographic imaging (based on clinical features and/or lab 
findings indicating metastatic disease)
• Abdominal US and CT: assesses lymph node involvement and extent of 
metastatic disease
• Skeletal scintigraphy: detects bone metastases
• Cranial MRI: detects CNS metastases
• Whole body PET-CT: best method for detection of occult disease
How to stage lung cancer

NSCLC
• Stage 1, 2, 3a  Curative
• Surgical resection +/- adjuvant chemo
• If surgery not possible  chemo + radiation  consider sx if tumor shrinks
• Stage 3b, 4  Palliative (symptom oriented)
SCLC
• Limited disease  curative
• Chemo + radiation
• Prophylactic cranial irradiation in patients who respond to initial chemo 
• Extensive disease  palliative

--- SLIDE 53 ---


--- SLIDE 54 ---


--- SLIDE 55 ---
Source: UpToDate, Amboss
Medical therapy
• Polychemotherapy e.g. SCLC: cisplatin and etoposide
• Targeted therapy when tumor has positive cytological markers: 
EGFR inhibitors, ALK tyrosine kinase inhibitors
Surgical management
• Open thoracic surgery VS. VATS 
• Lobectomy (standard approach): resection of one lobe
• If FEV1 > 1.5 L and DLCO > 60%: lobectomy can be tolerated
• Wedge resection for patients with small, localized tumors who cannot 
tolerate lobectomy
• Pneumonectomy: complete lung resection in the case of a central 
tumor

• Cessation of smoking

5–10 years. 
• After 15–20 yrs risk continues to decrease but always 
remains slightly elevated vs a non-smoker.
• Screening 2025
• low-dose CT in patients aged 50–80 (previously 55-74) 
years (USPSTF recommends 55–80 years) with either:
• ≥ 20 pack yrs (previously 30) in current smokers or those
