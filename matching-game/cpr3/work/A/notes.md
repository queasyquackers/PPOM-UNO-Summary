# Author A notes (lecture conflicts, decisions)

## Decisions so far
- Antifungals: CV136/CV137 teach antifungals only as indications; mechanism cards rest on CV136's "ergosterol is the therapy target" (summary: polyenes bind it, azoles block its synthesis). Itraconazole and voriconazole have generic azole mechanism cards, so they carry `avoid` on mechanism with fluconazole and each other.
- Vancomycin: CV128 slide 29 places it at the D-Ala step; D-Ala-D-Lac resistance appears only in the CV138 summary, not the lecture, so it is left off.
- Doxycycline: mechanism/AE come from the CV128 Rapid Pharm Concept Review (tetracycline, 30S, cation binding, teeth/bone). Uses from CV131 (Q fever), CV130 (Mycoplasma), CV141 (CAP).
- Rifapentine in the 4-month regimen: Slide 8 says daily, the lecturer says weekly; the use card names the regimen without a frequency.
- Clofazimine and other CV128 drugs follow slides 12-43; no conflicts with board convention beyond the summary's noted ones (ethambutol cell wall vs membrane; macrolide translocation vs peptidyl transferase; pretomanid target) - cards say "cell envelope" and "translocation" (slide 36 + lecturer).
- CONFLICT CV162 thiazides: the recording says thiazides "increase urinary calcium excretion"; Slide 28 gives no mechanism, and the school's diuretic lecture (CV31) teaches the opposite (thiazides retain calcium, lowering urine calcium). Plainly wrong as spoken, so the mechanism card states the standard direction (less calcium reaches the urine); the use card follows Slide 28.
- Potassium citrate: lecture says "citrate" (Slide 28-29) without naming the salt; item named potassium citrate as the usual prescribed form. Urine-alkalinizing agents for uric acid and cystine stones are not named in the lecture, so they are not added.
- Supplemental O2: CV126 explains CO2 retention by the hypoxic-drive theory (dated); card says "taught as loss of hypoxic drive" and keeps the safe instruction (titrate), true either way.
- DATED CV138 gonorrhea: the lecture (Slide 13) teaches CDC dual therapy, IM ceftriaxone + oral azithromycin; current CDC guidance is ceftriaxone alone, adding doxycycline when chlamydia is not excluded (the CV152 summary already says doxycycline). Azithromycin's card says "chlamydia cover beside IM ceftriaxone", which holds either way; ceftriaxone's card just says "IM dose for gonorrhea".
- Pertussis: CV130 and CV138 teach macrolides for pertussis but do not tie timing to the catarrhal stage (that is only in the CV138 summary), so the card leaves the stage out.
- Not written as correlates: the CV128 Question Review amikacin nephrotoxicity case (MDR-TB, BUN/creatinine up) - E owns nephrotoxic acute tubular injury, so a second ATI vignette would collide; the CV128 Rapid Pharm Concept Review chloramphenicol gray-baby case - the summary marks it outside the deck and chloramphenicol is not taught for any block infection. Isoniazid peripheral neuropathy WAS added (Question Review case 1).
- WAGR: slide and lecturer call it autosomal dominant (conflicts with a deletion syndrome); the gene card says only "WT1 deletion, 11p13", no inheritance.
- Alport: Slide 42-43 "X-linked dominant (85%)"; card follows the slide.
- Gene merges: ALK + ROS1 (one adenocarcinoma item; lecture lists them together with no separate facts); complement factor I + CD46 (one aHUS item); TSC1/TSC2 one item; TERT/TERC one item.
- Cross-check against B-F fragments (crosscheck.py): no FAIL/WARN involving A's names; no other author wrote drug-toxicity vignettes.
