# F notes - lecture conflicts and decisions

## CV126
- Toxic alcohols: the lecture says methanol's "major toxicities are renal and retinal"; board convention puts
  kidney injury (oxalate) on ethylene glycol and vision on methanol. The lecture never ties kidney failure to
  ethylene glycol. Cards agree with both: methanol card = vision loss; ethylene glycol card = coolant source,
  normal vision; no kidney finding on either. No oxalate crystals (not taught).
- Toxic alcohol management cards differ only by thiamine + pyridoxine (ethylene glycol cofactors) -> mutual
  avoid on management. No workup cards for either (lecture gives only the anion gap, identical for both).
- Rhabdo bicarbonate: lecture says no bicarb (fewer casts, no change in AKI/mortality) - card follows lecture.
- Hyperventilation K+ shift: lecturer ties hypokalemia to "a lot of bicarb" in respiratory alkalosis (HCO3 falls
  in that disorder per his own Slide 9). Card states only low K+, not the explanation.
- ARDS: Berlin PEEP >=5 requirement vs his "measure off treatment" - card gives the ratio only.
- TRALI is a cause of ARDS -> avoid on presentation between the two (a TRALI vignette is technically ARDS).
- Added "Lactic acidosis from septic shock" (MUDPILES "most common", the lecture's own anion-gap MCQ, case 4).
  Septic source = leg cellulitis so it cannot be read as pyelonephritis (mine) or pneumonia (D).
- Severe COVID-19 with ARDS is D's item; my ARDS vignette uses pancreatitis (an indirect cause he lists) so it
  cannot be read as COVID. Cardiogenic pulmonary edema is C's (CV146): my ARDS card has flat neck veins, no
  edema, wedge 10, normal LV, no effusions.
- Acute mountain sickness: lecture teaches only "very low O2 tension" + prophylactic acetazolamide. Symptoms
  (headache, nausea, insomnia) are generic vignette scaffolding.

## CV159
- Anemia of CKD: the lecture teaches only the work-up (start below Hgb 11/12, iron studies first, EPO level only
  if confounding) - no treatment, so no management card (ESA is A's drug).
- Uremic complications (pericarditis/encephalopathy/bleeding): not taught as a separate case - bruising,
  confusion and somnolence are in the uremia symptom list. Folded into "Advanced CKD with volume overload and
  uremia" (merged with CV143's CKD/volume overload/uremia block). Not a separate item.
- Advanced CKD mgmt: lecture says avoid ACE inhibitors in stage 4-5; the hyperkalemia vignette is therefore stage
  3b so its "the ACEi may not need to stop" line does not contradict.
- Hyperkalemia in CKD kept as a case (mechanism + management on Slide 23, ACEi/ARB monitoring Slide 39, CV126
  bicarbonate shift). Peaked T waves etc. not taught -> not on the card.
- Contrast-associated AKI vs E's nephrotoxic acute tubular injury (CV155): possible overlap if E's ATI card
  uses contrast; my card is built on the CKD + unheld metformin/NSAID/diuretic set-up. Orchestrator may want an
  avoid between them.

## CV152 / CV153
- The CV152 summary file contains board-review content not in the CV152 transcript (praziquantel, putty kidney,
  fetal-head sign, fosfomycin, prostatic massage contraindication, 4-6 weeks for prostatitis, 'profuse vs scant'
  discharge). Cards follow the transcripts/slides only. Durations from CV153: prostatitis 2-4 weeks (not 4-6).
- CV152 summary says cystitis first-line = nitrofurantoin/TMP-SMX/fosfomycin; CV153 (the clinical lecture) says
  TMP-SMX 3 days, sulfa allergy -> nitrofurantoin 5-7 d or FQ 3 d, and she often uses FQ for local resistance.
  Card follows CV153.
- Asymptomatic bacteriuria in pregnancy vs "UTI in pregnancy": the pregnancy card is a screen-detected
  bacteriuria at 14 weeks (the lecture's teaching point: all UTIs in pregnancy treated) -> avoid on
  presentation with Asymptomatic bacteriuria (whose vignette is a non-pregnant older diabetic).
- CAUTI: neither lecture teaches CAUTI treatment (CV153 never says when/how long; only an un-walked algorithm)
  -> no management card. Organism features (oxidase+, non-lactose fermenter, biofilm) from CV152.
- Urethritis: no treatment taught in CV152/153 (only "rule out STI") -> no management card. Kept as ONE item:
  neither lecture separates gonococcal from nongonococcal clinically (only by organism, which is B's).
- Chronic bacterial prostatitis kept (NIH table + "70% sterile urine, test for chlamydia"); chronic pelvic pain
  syndrome not made an item (one table row).
- Candiduria, GU TB, urinary schistosomiasis, viral hemorrhagic cystitis: CV152 teaches these only as organisms
  (2-3 sentences each; CV157 lists them as cystitis causes). Kept as lean presentation + workup items, no
  management (praziquantel, RIPE, cidofovir, fluconazole are not taught in these lectures). Sterile pyuria for
  GU TB is vignette scaffolding (not said in CV152). Candiduria avoids CAUTI on presentation.
- Viral hemorrhagic cystitis renamed "(immunocompromised)" - "after transplant" would ban the word transplant
  from its own card.

  and keeps prophylaxis for grades 1-2 with recurrent infection (the speaker's placement).
- VUR definition conflict: speaker = urine shoots back up the ureter; slide = "failure of the bladder to empty".
  Card follows the speaker (and CV157).

- Added Physiologic phimosis ("phimosis is the normal one; paraphimosis is the bad one") as the paraphimosis
  foil. Added Torsion of the appendix testis (taught with its own slide and management). Epididymitis is only
  listed in the differential -> not an item.

  actions). Lecture ages: primary is a problem after 6; secondary = dry by 4-5 then wet.

  first-morning-void test). Pediatric hematuria workup is a list of causes, not one diagnosis -> no item.

- HSP: lecture says platelets normal and "sterile pyuria"; biopsy findings not taught on CV161 (IgA deposits

## CV162
- Merged "Hydronephrosis" into "Renal colic from an obstructing ureteral calculus": the lecture's hydronephrosis
  block is the CT picture of an obstructing stone; a stand-alone hydronephrosis vignette could not be told from
  colic (or from PUV / UPJ / BPH elsewhere).
- "Infected obstructing stone" renamed "Obstructive pyelonephritis (infected, blocked kidney)" (a name with
  "stone" bans the word from its own cards); avoid on presentation with Acute pyelonephritis.
- Staghorn calculi block merged into "Struvite stones (infection stones)".
- Added "Calcium phosphate stones" - one of the four stone types in the lecture's master table, with its own
  pH, crystal (wedge-shaped prism), nephrocalcinosis link and citrate treatment. Vignette uses RTA type 1
  (named by the lecture as raising urine calcium and causing hypocitraturia).
- Thiazide mechanism: recording says thiazides "increase urinary calcium excretion" (wrong; CV31 teaches they
  lower it). Cards say only "add a thiazide" - no mechanism.
- Calcium oxalate pH: Slide 7 lists low urine pH as a cause, Slide 41 says largely pH-independent. No pH on the
  calcium oxalate cards.
- Imaging sequence conflict (spoken: x-ray first; slide: non-contrast CT first). Colic workup shows a CT.
- 10 mm edge: Slide 26 ">= 10" vs everywhere else "> 10" - card uses ">10 mm".
- Idiopathic hypercalciuria: the lecture names "hypercalciuria" only as a metabolic risk factor; the item is
  built from that plus the calcium-stone prevention teaching (low sodium, normal calcium, thiazide). Its
  management card and the enteric hyperoxaluria one share the calcium-stone rules -> mutual avoid on mgmt.
  Their workup cards WARN (0.61 overlap) but differ in the separating values (urine calcium vs oxalate).
- Crystals in plain words: CaOx "small square crystals with an X across them", CaP "wedge-shaped prism",
  struvite "rectangular prism-shaped", uric acid "rhomboid plates / rosettes", cystine "flat six-sided".
- Medullary sponge kidney: no management taught -> none on the card.
- Potassium citrate / acetohydroxamic acid / named cystine chelators are not spoken -> cards say "alkalinize the
  urine" and "chelating agent".

## CV163
- BPH: CV163 teaches mechanism (androgen nodules + alpha tone), urge leakage and eventual retention; CV157 the
  thick, ridged (trabeculated) bladder wall. Neither teaches BPH drugs (alpha blockers / 5-ARIs not spoken in
  CV163) -> no management card.
- Reflex incontinence: lecture gives only the lesion level (suprapontine or suprasacral) and causes (stroke,
  Parkinson's, tumor, neurosurgery); no definition or treatment. Vignette = suprasacral cord injury (per
  ASSIGNMENTS), separated from urge by absent sensation/warning. No management card.
- Urge vs overflow: the lecture names MS and diabetic neuropathy for BOTH. Urge card uses an idiopathic
  older woman; overflow card the diabetic with a large residual. Reflex card = cord injury.
- Overflow mechanism: Slide 25's last bullet ("because of a contracting detrusor") contradicts its first bullet
  and the speech; card follows impaired contractility.
- Brainstem: spoken "relaxes the detrusor", slide "detrusor contraction" - not on any card.
- Drug-induced incontinence dropped as an item: the lecture gives a drug table, not a case; the drugs sit on
  the functional (sedative), overflow and retention cards.
- Added "Nocturnal incontinence in an older adult (fluid shifts)" - a type in the lecture's table with its own
  workup (voiding diary) and management (fluid timing, diuretic in the morning, leg elevation, DDAVP caution).
- Cauda equina: lecture attributes it to herniation "cutting off the S5 nerve root" (odd/incomplete); card
  states only the clinical picture and the emergency. Absent ankle jerks are vignette scaffolding.
- Urinary retention (CV143 bladder exam: distention, suprapubic tenderness, dullness) built as acute
  drug-induced retention without leakage; avoid on workup with Overflow incontinence (both show a big bladder).
