## Lecture conflicts and how the cards handle them

- **IgA nephropathy EM, GBM thickness:** CV147 says GBM of normal thickness; CV149 (Slide 11
  narration) says the adjacent basement membrane is thickened and distorted. Cards state only
  "deposits within the mesangial matrix; endothelium intact" - no thickness claim.
- **IgA nephropathy, ESRD share:** CV149 speaker 50%, slide 25-50%. Not on any card.
- **PSGN EM, GBM reaction:** Slide 14 "w/o significant" reaction, speaker "will be significant".
  Cards say nothing about GBM reaction; they describe large discrete subepithelial deposits.
- **PSGN treatment:** CV149 Slide 18 says symptomatic only; speaker mentions antibiotics; CV161
  says antibiotics do not prevent PSGN. Management card follows CV161 + Slide 18
  (supportive; antibiotics do not prevent the kidney disease).
- **Lupus nephritis burden:** speaker 25%, slide 75%. Not on any card.
- **Alport inheritance:** CV149 summary: speaker says AR then "X-linked recessive"; Slide 42
  prints X-linked dominant (85%). Card uses "X-linked COL4A5 in 85%" without dominant/recessive.
- **MPGN foot processes / mesangium (CV147, CV148):** not used.
- **Amyloid fibril location (CV148):** cards say only "fibrils 8-10 nm" deposited in the
  mesangium (Slide 27: deposits in and disrupt the mesangium).
- **Thin basement membrane disease LM:** no lecture gives LM; lightMicro is a required field,
  so the card says "unremarkable glomeruli; no proliferation, no interstitial foam cells"
  (the contrast with Alport that CV149 draws) and `avoid`s MCD and lupus I-II on lightMicro.
- **Amyloid IF:** CV148 gives no IF, only immunohistochemistry (lambda+, kappa-). That
  immunostain result sits on the immunofluor card, labelled "Immunostain".

## Design decisions

- Lupus nephritis split by class for the glomerular matrix as CV149's table does, but
  classes I+II merged (both mesangial-only, separated only by "normal" vs "mesangial
  hypercellularity") and III+IV merged (identical IF/EM/clue; separated only by "some" vs
  "all" glomeruli). Correlates split proliferative (III-IV, nephritic) vs membranous (V,
  nephrotic), the clinical split CV149's stems make.
- Dense deposit disease cards avoid the words "dense" and "deposit" (name tokens); EM is
  written as "electron-opaque ribbon inside the GBM".
- Anti-GBM cards avoid any word starting "anti" (name token).
- Name-giveaway: "SLE" is not written on lupus cards; serology and features carry them.

## More conflicts / inferences on correlate cards

- **Atypical HUS workup "ADAMTS13 activity normal":** CV151 separates aHUS (complement
  regulators) from TTP (ADAMTS13 autoantibody) but never states aHUS ADAMTS13 is normal; the
  card states the implied contrast.
- **Squamous cell carcinoma of the bladder:** CV157 says primary squamous carcinoma is beyond
  the lecture, but gives Schistosoma -> 70% squamous; CV152 adds terminal-spined eggs and
  squamous bladder cancer. Workup says "keratinizing carcinoma" to describe squamous
  histology without the name (the name token "squamous" is banned on its own card).
- **Angiomyolipoma:** CV156 gives fat content (yellow-tan gross, empty fat cells, hyperechoic
  sonogram) and retroperitoneal hemorrhage; the card's "CT: mass containing fat" restates it.
- **NSAID papillary necrosis:** CV155 says high-dose NSAIDs cause AKI "including papillary
  necrosis" with no clinical detail; card adds "visible blood in her urine" only.
- **Typical HUS antibiotics:** CV161 (no antibiotics, toxin release) vs CV151 (dialysis if
  renal failure) - no conflict, card combines them.
- **Malignant hypertension:** CV151 slide says papilledema "has to be present", speaker says
  only that doctors like to find it - card includes swollen discs as one finding among many.
- **PSGN age:** CV149 Slide 13 "children 2-6"; the CV149 and CV161 stems use 10- and
  12-year-olds. Clue says 2-6 (slide), presentation uses the 10-year-old stem.
