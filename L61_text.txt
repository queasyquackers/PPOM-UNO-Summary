History Taking

--- Page 4 ---
• What other medical problems exist?
• Psych history- anti psychotic use in the past
• Cardiac? Diabetes? Cancer? GERD?
• Was an anti-emetic used long term?
• This is also important in terms of medication use
Goal: rule out secondary/drug-induced parkinsonism
Medical History

--- Page 5 ---
• What medications are currently being used?
• Certain medications- steroids, valproic acid, lithium may 
cause tremor.
• Dopamine blockers → parkinsonism
• Levodopa → on/off periods
• Dopamine agonists → impulse control, sleep attacks
• Remember to make a note of allergies to any 
medications.
Current and past medication use

--- Page 6 ---
• Does anyone have PD in the family?
• If so, at what age did the symptoms begin?
• Did anyone have a tremor?
• Use a cane, walker or wheelchair to get 
around?
Most PD is sporadic; however, family history 
raises suspicion for genetic forms.
Family History

--- Page 7 ---
(PD Nexus, 2019)
• 15% of patients have family history
• Individuals of Ashkenazi Jewish descent are at a higher risk
• Most common genes: LRRK2, GBA1, PRKN
• Less common genes: SNCA, PINK1, PARK7, VPS35
• Core mechanisms: alpha-synuclein aggregation, mitochondrial 
dysfunction, impaired protein degradation
• Genetic Testing considered when: age of onset <50 and/or strong family 
history
• Even in genetic form of PD,environmental exposures strongly influence 
when and whether the disease develops.
Genetics of PD

• Military service (i.e. agent orange in Vietnam/camp lejeune)
• Live/work near farm (sprayed pesticides)

(Roundup)

Cranial Nerves

--- Page 16 ---
• In addition to strength, check the tone.
• Lead-pipe rigidity
• Cogwheeling
• Do not forget to compare rigidity from side to 
side.
• Check for rapid alternating movements
• Diagnosis requires bradykinesia with rigidity 
(+/- tremor)
Motor Exam

• Resting, action, postural?
• Resting → Parkinson’s
• Postural/action → essential tremor

for falls?
• Check reflexes: including plantar and 
Hoffmann

• Especially look for arm swing

• Weight bearing

• Shuffling steps
• Stooped posture

(Mayo Clinic, 2023)
• Clinical diagnosis- physical and neurological examination
• Dopamine transporter (DAT) scan
• Genetic testing- useful if there is known family history or have 
early-onset disease

dopaminergic therapy

--- Page 22 ---
A 61-year-old man presents with progressive difficulty walking and slowness of 
movement. On physical exam, he has decreased facial expression, reduced left arm 
swing, cogwheel rigidity of the left upper extremity, and decreased blink rate. Patient 
has left resting tremor as well. He also reports loss of smell for several years as well 
as vivid dreams which he acts out. Which finding is most essential to establish the 
diagnosis of Parkinson’s disease in this patient?
 A. Loss of smell
 B. Masked facies
 C. Bradykinesia
 D. Resting tremor
 E. REM sleep behavior disorder
Practice Question 1
Explanation: Parkinson’s disease requires 
bradykinesia for diagnosis. Resting tremor is not 
necessary for diagnosis of PD. Anosmia and REM 
sleep behavior disorder are both prodromal 
features, but they do not establish the diagnosis.

posture, reduced arm swing, shuffling steps, and turns around using multiple small 
steps. When asked to start walking, he hesitates and feels as though his feet are 
“stuck to the floor.” Which underlying neurologic dysfunction best explains this 
disease?
   A. Corticospinal tract lesion
--> spasticity
   B. Cerebellar hemispheric dysfunction-->wide-based, ataxic gait
   C. Basal ganglia dysfunction 
   D. Dorsal column dysfunction--> sensory ataxia, positive Romberg
   E. Vestibular system dysfunction--> imbalance, vertigo
Practice Question 2
Explanation: Shuffling, freezing, en 
bloc turning, and difficulty starting 
movement points to a dysfunction in 
the basal ganglia, since it controls 
voluntary movements and motor 
initiation.
